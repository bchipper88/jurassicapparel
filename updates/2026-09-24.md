## Why that one — and why not the one the queue named

Yesterday's queue named **`dinosaur onesie adult`** for today. I skipped it. The numbers were
fine — 1,900/mo, SD 24, an 8,100/mo October peak, all still true on re-verification. The
inventory was not.

`KEYWORDS.md` described that keyword as pointing "straight at the store's #1 traffic page,
*Realistic Jurassic Adult Dinosaur Pajamas*." I pulled that product before writing. **It is a
pair of unisex wide-leg pajama pants.** The store sells no adult one-piece onesie at all — I
checked every pajama, onesie and jumpsuit SKU in the catalog.

A shopper searching `dinosaur onesie adult` wants a hooded kigurumi. Sending them to a page
that offers trousers is the kind of thing that bounces at 90%, earns nothing, and quietly
teaches Google the page is a bad result. It also breaks charter principle 2 outright. So it is
logged `⏭️ Skipped` with the full reasoning, and the lesson is recorded in the queue: **verify
inventory at selection time, not at writing time.**

## What I found

**1. There is no `dinosaur-pajamas` collection, and that is the cheapest win available.**
1,600/mo at SD 17 with the highest CPC in the queue, already ranking #14 — served by a single
product page, because no collection page exists. Three ACTIVE SKUs are scattered across the
catalog with nothing grouping them. A commercial collection URL is a far better ranking asset
for a transactional term than one product page. **Ten minutes of owner time.** `BACKLOG.md` #12.
Today's article is written to link into it the moment it exists.

**2. Two duplicate "Dinosaur Christmas Pajamas" collections, both invisible to customers.**
`dinosaur-christmas-pajamas` and `dinosaur-christmas-pajamas-1` — identical titles, 7 products
each, and every one of those products is DRAFT with 0 inventory. Both render as empty pages
while staying crawlable. This is almost certainly feeding the HIGH-impact duplicate-title and
duplicate-meta flags in the site audit. `BACKLOG.md` #13.

*This is not a re-flag of BACKLOG #0.* The products being in draft is your decision and it
stands. The problem is the two duplicate empty collection pages that exist regardless.

**3. A real bug in my own link checker — now fixed.** `check-links.py` enforced "never link an
empty collection" by reading product *counts*. `dinosaur-christmas-pajamas` has a count of 7
and would have sailed through, while rendering empty to every customer who clicked. The rule
was only ever enforceable against collections empty in the snapshot, not ones effectively empty
because their members are unpublished. Both handles are now blocklisted in `data/catalog.json`
and I verified the checker fails on them. The durable fix — count only ACTIVE products in the
weekly catalog refresh — is scheduled into the Monday routine. `BACKLOG.md` #14.

**4. A second bug, in the publish script.** `md-to-shopify.py` hardcoded the tags
`["dinosaur costume", "halloween", "family costume", "dinosaur apparel"]` onto *every* article.
Day 1 was a costume article so nobody noticed; today's pajama article would have gone live
tagged "halloween" and "family costume". Tags now come from the article's own front matter.

**5. `Dino Nuggies – Women's Pajama Shorts` is DRAFT with 59,994 units available**, while its
near-identical sibling is ACTIVE and selling. Not a Christmas product, so BACKLOG #0 doesn't
cover it — it looks like an oversight. Publishing it doubles the pajama-shorts range at zero
cost, right before the Nov–Dec sleepwear peak. Two minutes. `BACKLOG.md` #15.
