How the page is built:
- **An at-a-glance table** up top, with six questions and short answers.
- **Count** (about 50–60, and why there's no single number), **size** (about 12 in including root; the root is most of
  it), and **shape** (thick "killer bananas", serrations, the crack-stopping chambers in the serrations, deep roots).
- **The fossil evidence:** Triceratops bite marks, the healed Edmontosaurus tail with a tooth tip in it, bone-packed
  coprolite, and the ~35,000 N bite-force estimate (hedged, other estimates run higher).
- **Replacement** (about two years per tooth, from growth-line counts; Nigersaurus as the contrast) and the **2023 lips
  study**, with the disagreement stated.
- **A comparison table** against Allosaurus, Spinosaurus, Triceratops, Edmontosaurus, Nigersaurus, a great white shark
  and a human.
- **Ten facts for kids**, a **"how to tell a real T. rex tooth"** section (locality, restoration, ID, casts; US
  federal-land rule), and a seven-question FAQ.

I left out anything I couldn't pin down: fossil prices, exact per-jaw tooth counts, and crown lengths. Every number
in the page is one that's widely cited, and the estimates are labelled as estimates.

## Why that one

The planned target failed (below), so I ran a discovery pass off a `t rex` seed. `t rex teeth` was the best result by a
distance: 17× the volume of the planned target at half its difficulty, and no live post has "teeth" or "tooth" in its
title. The SERP (collected 2026-11-08, read free, not refreshed) is a mix of fossil sellers (minimuseum DA 45, eBay,
dinofossils DA 18) and single-angle explainers. Wikipedia covers dinosaur teeth in general, fossilageminerals (DA 31)
is at #5, thecitrinecircle (DA 21) at #13, plus Smithsonian and iflscience. None of them answers count, size, shape,
replacement, evidence, lips and buying in one place.
