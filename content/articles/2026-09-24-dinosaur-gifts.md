## What makes a dinosaur gift fail

Worth naming, because the failure modes are consistent and easy to avoid:

**It's a pun, not an object.** "Tea-Rex" is funny once. If the joke is the entire product,
the gift has a shelf life of about four seconds. The test: strip the wordplay away — is there
still something worth owning underneath?

**It's sized and you guessed.** Apparel is the highest-risk category in this whole guide, and
it's worth being blunt about why: these products are made to order, so all sales are final.
There's no casual exchange for the next size up. If you haven't seen the person's size written
down somewhere, buy from the unsized shelf — mugs, totes, blankets, puzzles, stickers, phone
cases, socks.

**It's aimed at the wrong age.** A dinosaur gift for a three-year-old and one for a nine-year-old
have almost nothing in common. Buying up reads as boring; buying down reads as babyish. The
age breakdown above exists for exactly this reason.

**It's for a dinosaur fan generally, not this dinosaur fan.** Most people who like dinosaurs
have a favourite, and they will tell you about it at length if asked. Asking is not a spoiler —
"which one's your favourite again?" is a normal question that nobody reads as gift research.

**What are good dinosaur gifts for a 3 year old?**
Something wearable. Three is the age where the dinosaur becomes an identity, and they want it
*on* them — a shirt, a hoodie, shoes. The [kids' collection](/collections/kids-dinosaur-apparel)
is the place to start, and buy a size up.

**Are there dinosaur gifts for adults that aren't a joke?**
Yes, and the test is simple: would they have bought this object anyway? A blanket, a tote bag,
a phone case and a good mug all pass. A novelty item with a pun on it usually doesn't — those
are cards, not gifts.
