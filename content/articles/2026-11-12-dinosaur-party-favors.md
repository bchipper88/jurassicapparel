# Dinosaur Party Favors Kids Actually Keep: Ideas by Budget and Age

You've planned the games, ordered the cake, and now there's a stack of empty paper bags on the counter. Dinosaur party
favors are the last job before the party and the easiest one to overspend on. Most favor bags end up as a handful of
small plastic bits that break in the car or get lost under a seat.

The good news: kids don't need much. One thing they like, one thing they made, and something sweet is plenty. Here are
dinosaur party favor ideas sorted by budget and by age, with what to skip, how many to make and when to order.

## The quick answer: one keeper, one thing they made, one treat

If you only read one section, read this. A favor bag that kids actually keep has three parts:

1. **One keeper.** A single good thing beats ten cheap ones. A waterproof dinosaur sticker for a water bottle, a small
   dinosaur figure, or a coloring page they'll actually use.
2. **One thing they made at the party.** A dug-up "fossil," a decorated crown, a painted dino egg. Kids are proudest of
   these, and they cost you almost nothing.
3. **One small treat.** A bag of "dino eggs" (chocolate eggs or jelly beans) or "fossil" cookies. Check with parents
   about allergies first.

That's it. You can stop there, or pick and mix from the ideas below.

## What to put in dinosaur goodie bags (and what to skip)

**Good fillers:**

- **One dinosaur sticker per child.** Let them pick from a few designs if you can. A [Green T-Rex](/products/green-t-rex-sticker),
  a soft [Baby Dino watercolor](/products/baby-dino-watercolor-sticker) and a
  [Brontosaurus Unicorn](/products/brontosaurus-unicorn-dinosaur-sticker) cover most tastes. Kids love choosing their
  own. There are more in our [dinosaur stickers collection](/collections/dinosaur-stickers).
- **A coloring page, rolled up with two crayons** and tied with string or a strip of green tissue. It looks like a
  scroll, and it gives the ride home something to do. The designs are in our
  [dinosaur printables](/collections/dinosaur-printables).
- **A small dinosaur figure.** One decent figure is better than a bag of tiny ones. For a big group, buy a mixed tub and
  let each child pick one at the door.
- **Something they made.** See the DIY ideas below.
- **A treat with a dinosaur name.** "Dino eggs" (chocolate eggs, malted milk balls, jelly beans), "fossil" cookies
  pressed with a toy dinosaur's feet, or "lava" fruit snacks.

**Skip these:**

- **Bags of tiny plastic toys.** They break, they scatter, and parents quietly throw them out.
- **Whistles, kazoos and anything loud.** The other parents will remember who sent it home.
- **Slime and putty for young kids.** It ends up in hair and carpet.
- **Small parts for under-threes.** If there are toddlers or baby siblings at the party, keep a separate set of bags
  with nothing small enough to swallow. Check the age label on anything you buy.
- **Candy as the whole bag.** One treat is a favor. A bag full of candy is a problem you've handed to someone else.

## DIY dinosaur party favors

These double as party activities, so the favor gets made during the party instead of the night before.

**Salt-dough fossils.** Mix 2 cups flour, 1 cup salt and about ¾ cup water into a dough. Give each child a ball, have
them press it flat and stamp a toy dinosaur's feet or a plastic skeleton into it. Send them home soft in a zip bag
with a note: bake low (around 200°F) for 2–3 hours to harden, or let them air-dry for a few days.

**Dino egg hatching.** Wrap a small dinosaur figure in a ball of play dough or a baking soda and water paste, let it
dry, and let kids "hatch" it with a spoon or a squirt of vinegar. The figure goes home in the bag. This is also a great
party game. See our [dinosaur party games](/blogs/blog/dinosaur-party-games) for how to run a fossil dig and an egg hunt
for a whole group.

**Decorate-your-own crown.** Cut paper crowns with spikes along the top, and put out stickers, crayons and glue. Every
child wears theirs for the photo and takes it home.

**Fossil cookies.** Bake plain sugar cookies and press a clean toy dinosaur's feet into each one before baking. Wrap
two per child. These are the favor parents eat on the way home.

**Seed "dino eggs."** Roll wildflower seeds into balls of damp shredded paper, let them dry, and label them
"plant a dinosaur forest." Best for spring and summer parties.

## How many dinosaur party favors to make

- **Make two or three more than your RSVP count.** Siblings show up. Someone brings a cousin.
- **Make a smaller set for siblings** if you know younger ones are coming, with nothing small.
- **Don't forget the birthday kid.** They'll want a bag too, even though everything at the party is theirs.
- **Hand them out at the door on the way out**, not during the party. Bags opened mid-party get lost and fought over.

## Dinosaur party favors FAQ

**What are good dinosaur party favors?**
One thing kids will keep (a waterproof dinosaur sticker, a small figure or a coloring page), one thing they made at the
party (a fossil or a crown), and one small treat. Quality beats quantity.

**How much should I spend on party favors per child?**
There's no rule, and kids don't notice the price. Every idea on this page fits in $3 to $10 per child. A printable coloring page and
something made at the party can get you close to free.

**What do you put in a dinosaur goodie bag?**
A sticker, a coloring page rolled up with crayons, a small dinosaur figure, something they made at the party, and one
dinosaur-named treat like "dino eggs." Skip small plastic toys and noisemakers.

**Are coloring pages good party favors?**
Yes, especially as a printable. You buy the file once and print as many as you need. Roll each page up with two
crayons and tie it with string, and it looks like a gift.

**Can I give stickers to toddlers?**
Stickers can be a choking hazard for very young children. For under-threes, give the sticker to the parent, or skip it
and choose a board book or a large soft toy.

**Should party favors be given at a birthday party?**
They're not required, but they're a nice thank-you, and they help end the party on a happy note. Hand them out at the
door as guests leave.
