# Allosaurus vs T. Rex: Size, Bite and Who Would Win (They Never Met)

Someone in your house has lined up a plastic Allosaurus against a plastic T. rex and wants to know who wins. The honest
answer to **Allosaurus vs T. rex** has a twist: the two never met. *Allosaurus* lived about 155 to 145 million years
ago, in the Late Jurassic. *Tyrannosaurus rex* lived about 68 to 66 million years ago, at the very end of the
Cretaceous. That leaves a gap of roughly **80 million years** between them, which is longer than the time between
T. rex and you.

If they *had* met, a full-grown T. rex would almost certainly have won. It was about a third longer than the biggest Allosaurus,
around four times heavier, and had one of the strongest bites of any land animal. Allosaurus was the lighter, longer-armed, quicker
hunter of its own world. Below: the side-by-side numbers, what each one did best, how a fight would really go on the
evidence, and why the movies keep putting them in the same scene.

## They missed each other by about 80 million years

This is the fact most fight videos skip, and it's the best one. Allosaurus is a Jurassic dinosaur. Its neighbors were
*Stegosaurus*, *Brachiosaurus*, *Diplodocus* and *Apatosaurus*. T. rex lived alongside *Triceratops*, *Edmontosaurus*
and *Ankylosaurus*, near the end of the age of dinosaurs.

Here's a way to make the gap real at the kitchen table:

- T. rex died out about **66 million years ago**.
- Allosaurus lived about **150 million years ago**.
- 150 − 66 = **84 million years** between them. That's more than the 66 million years between the last T. rex and us.

So T. rex is closer in time to your kid than it is to Allosaurus. A Jurassic Allosaurus would have found the world of
T. rex as unfamiliar as we would, with flowering plants, different mammals and different prey.

## They weren't close cousins, either

Both were big two-legged meat-eaters, the group called **theropods**, so people often picture Allosaurus as an early
version of T. rex. It wasn't. The two belong to different branches of the theropod family tree:

- **Allosaurus** was a **carnosaur**. Its branch later produced giants like *Carcharodontosaurus* and
  *Giganotosaurus* in the Cretaceous of Africa and South America.
- **T. rex** was a **coelurosaur**, the same branch that includes *Velociraptor* and, eventually, birds. Its early
  tyrannosaur relatives were small, lightly built animals living in Allosaurus's time. The giant tyrannosaurs didn't
  appear until tens of millions of years later.

So when a kid says "a T. rex is basically a big Allosaurus," the fair reply is that a T. rex is closer kin to a
chicken.

## Allosaurus size vs T. rex size

**Allosaurus size** is the question that usually sets up the comparison, and the answer surprises people: Allosaurus
was big, but nowhere near T. rex.

- **Length.** Most adult *Allosaurus fragilis* skeletons come out at about 25–28 feet (7.5–8.5 m). The biggest are
  around 30 feet (9 m). The biggest T. rex skeletons reach about 40 feet (12–13 m).
- **Weight.** Published estimates for Allosaurus vary widely, from under 1 tonne to about 4 tonnes, depending on the
  specimen and the method. A big adult was probably in the 1.5–2.5 tonne range. Big Al, a not-quite-grown animal,
  comes out at about **1.5 tonnes** (Bates et al., 2009). A big T. rex was about **8–9.5 tonnes**, so roughly four
  times heavier.
- **Height.** The biggest T. rex stood about 12 feet (3.7 m) at the hip. Allosaurus was clearly shorter, though hip
  height estimates for it vary more than the length figures do.

Picture it this way: Allosaurus was about the length of a city bus but built like a long, lean greyhound of a predator.
T. rex was that bus plus a small car, and built like a tank.

One caveat. A few very large Allosaurus-type bones have turned up in Oklahoma and New Mexico. They were long called
*Saurophaganax*, and some researchers now treat them as a big Allosaurus species. Even the largest of these were well
short of an adult T. rex.

## Skull and bite: slicer vs crusher

This is where the two were most different, and it shaped how each one killed.

### Allosaurus: wide gape, hatchet strike

The Allosaurus skull was long, narrow and lighter than you'd expect, with a low horn in front of each eye. When
Emily Rayfield and colleagues ran an engineering-style stress analysis on it in 2001, they found something odd. The
skull was very strong, able to take far more force than its jaw muscles could make. Its bite from muscle alone was
fairly weak for its size.

Their explanation: Allosaurus may have attacked like a **hatchet**. It opened its jaws very wide and drove the upper
teeth down into its prey with the strength of its neck and the weight of its body. Then it pulled back, slicing with
blade-like teeth.

A 2015 study by Stephan Lautenschlager backs this up. It found Allosaurus could open its mouth **about 79 to 92
degrees**, much wider than T. rex at **about 63.5 degrees**. That's the gape you want for a slashing bite into
something bigger than you.

### T. rex: the bone-crusher

T. rex did the opposite. Its skull was deep and broad, its teeth were thick, and its jaw muscles were enormous. Bite
force estimates for a big adult start around **35,000 newtons** (Gignac & Erickson, 2017), and some go well above
that (Bates & Falkingham, 2012). That's many times what Allosaurus could manage from its muscles. T. rex didn't need a
wide gape. It bit down and crushed, bone included. Fossil droppings full of bone chips prove it.

them, see the [T. rex skull guide](/blogs/blog/t-rex-skull).

## Arms: the one round Allosaurus wins

Everyone jokes about T. rex's arms. Allosaurus is the reason the joke works.

- **Allosaurus arms** were long and muscular, about a third of the length of its legs. Each hand had **three
  fingers tipped with large, hooked claws**. Those arms could grab and hold struggling prey.
- **T. rex arms** were about the length of a grown man's arm on an animal 40 feet long, with **two fingers**. They
  were strong for their size, but they couldn't reach its own mouth. In a fight they'd barely matter.

If the contest were arm-wrestling, Allosaurus would win easily.

## Speed and build

Nobody has a clocked top speed for either animal. Speed estimates for dinosaurs come from computer models of legs and
muscles, and from footprints. Keep any number you see as a best guess.

- **T. rex** was too heavy to truly run, according to several models. Hutchinson and Garcia (2002) found it could not
  have been a fast runner. A 2017 model by Sellers and colleagues put its top speed at about **12 mph (5 m/s)**, a
  fast walk with long strides. Our [T. rex footprint guide](/blogs/blog/t-rex-footprint) works through one real
  trackway.
- **Allosaurus** was much lighter for its length, with long legs. Most researchers expect it was quicker and more
  agile than an adult T. rex, but there's no well-agreed number.

Lightness came at a cost. Allosaurus fossils are famous for their injuries. Big Al, the near-complete Wyoming skeleton
found in 1991, has around **19 bones** that were broken, infected or diseased (Hanna, 2002). They include ribs, toes
and a foot that may have left it limping. Hunting big animals was dangerous.

## How Allosaurus hunted, and what it hunted

Allosaurus was the top predator of its world, and its world was full of giants:

- **Stegosaurus** fought back. One Allosaurus tail bone has a healed puncture wound that matches a Stegosaurus tail
  spike. A Stegosaurus neck plate has a bite-shaped notch that fits an Allosaurus snout (Carpenter et al., 2005). They
  really did fight, and both sides took damage.
- **Sauropods** like *Apatosaurus* were too big for one Allosaurus to tackle as adults. Young or sick ones were fair
  game, and Allosaurus may have fed on carcasses too.
- **Groups?** Dozens of Allosaurus have turned up together at the Cleveland-Lloyd Quarry in Utah, more than 40
  individuals. That's why you'll read about "pack hunting." But a pile of bones in one place doesn't prove they hunted
  together. They may have been trapped one by one, or gathered at the same food. The question is still open.

T. rex hunted big, armored and horned animals too, such as *Triceratops* and *Edmontosaurus*, and it also scavenged.
Its approach was the crushing bite. Allosaurus relied on the slash-and-wait strike.

## So, who would win: Allosaurus or T. rex?

Here's how the matchup looks if you take the fossil evidence seriously and ignore the movies.

**Grown-up vs grown-up: T. rex, nearly every time.** A big T. rex outweighed a big Allosaurus about four to one. Its
skull was built to take and deliver a crushing bite, and one good bite to the neck or ribs would likely end it.
Allosaurus's best weapon, the hatchet strike, was designed to wound big plant-eaters and wait them out. Against an
animal four times its weight that bites back, that's a poor plan.

**What Allosaurus had going for it:**
- Speed and agility, probably.
- Grasping arms with big claws, which T. rex didn't have.
- A wide gape and slicing teeth that could open long wounds.

**What T. rex had going for it:**
- About four times the weight.
- A bite many times stronger, with teeth that could break bone.
- A heavily built skull and neck designed to take the strain.
- Good forward-facing eyes and an excellent sense of smell.

**The fairer fight: Allosaurus vs a teenage T. rex.** Young T. rex were leggy, slimmer and faster, with thinner,
blade-like teeth. A big Allosaurus against a T. rex of about the same weight would be a far closer contest, and
Allosaurus's arms might decide it. That's still a made-up match, of course.

**The best real-world answer:** neither had to worry about the other. Allosaurus's real fights were with Stegosaurus,
and they left scars to prove it. T. rex's real rivals were *Triceratops* and other T. rex. Sue's skeleton has injuries
that some researchers think came from fights with its own kind.

## Why Jurassic World puts them together

If your kid is sure they've seen these two in the same movie, they have. *Jurassic World: Fallen Kingdom* (2018) brought
Allosaurus into the films, and it appears again in *Jurassic World Dominion* (2022). The
franchise mixes dinosaurs from different periods freely, which is part of the fun, and video games like *The Isle*
pit them against each other too.

It's a good moment to explain that "Jurassic Park" is mostly a Cretaceous park. T. rex, *Triceratops* and *Velociraptor*
are all Cretaceous animals. Allosaurus is one of the few real Jurassic stars.

## Allosaurus vs T. rex: facts for kids

1. **They never met.** About 80 million years separated them.
2. **T. rex lived closer to us than to Allosaurus.**
3. **Allosaurus means "different lizard."** Othniel Marsh named it in 1877 because its backbones looked different
   from other reptiles'.
4. **T. rex was about four times heavier.**
5. **Allosaurus had three big claws on each hand. T. rex had two small fingers.**
6. **Allosaurus could open its mouth wider.** Its gape may have reached about 90 degrees.
7. **T. rex had the much stronger bite.** It could crush bone.
8. **Allosaurus had little horns over its eyes.**
9. **Allosaurus is Utah's state fossil.**
10. **Big Al** is one of the most complete Allosaurus skeletons ever found, and it was covered in old injuries.

## FAQ

**Did Allosaurus and T. rex live at the same time?**
No. Allosaurus lived about 155–145 million years ago, in the Late Jurassic. T. rex lived about 68–66 million years ago,
at the end of the Cretaceous. They were about 80 million years apart.

**Which was bigger, Allosaurus or T. rex?**
T. rex, by a lot. The biggest T. rex were about 40 feet long and 8–9.5 tonnes. A big Allosaurus was about 28–30 feet
long and about 1.5–2.5 tonnes.

**Who would win in a fight, Allosaurus or T. rex?**
An adult T. rex would very likely win, thanks to its weight and bone-crushing bite. A big Allosaurus against a young
T. rex would be much closer.

**How big was Allosaurus?**
Most adults were about 25–28 feet (7.5–8.5 m) long, with the largest around 30 feet (9 m). A big adult weighed
about 1.5–2.5 tonnes.

**Was Allosaurus related to T. rex?**
Only distantly. Both were theropods, but Allosaurus was a carnosaur. T. rex was a coelurosaur, the group that includes
birds.

**Which had a stronger bite?**
T. rex, many times over. Allosaurus had a fairly weak bite from its muscles. It probably made up for it with a
wide-open, hatchet-like strike using its neck and body weight.

**Is Allosaurus in Jurassic World?**
Yes. It appears in *Jurassic World: Fallen Kingdom* (2018) and *Jurassic World Dominion* (2022).
