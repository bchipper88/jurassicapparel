# Dinosaur Pajamas for Adults: How to Find a Pair That Actually Fits

Search for dinosaur pajamas and you will get about forty results in size 4T. The internet
has decided that wanting a triceratops on your sleepwear means you are five years old,
and every big retailer has built its dinosaur category around toddlers. If you are an
adult who would quite like the same thing in your own size, the search gets frustrating
fast.

So here is the honest version. Adult dinosaur pajamas exist, there are fewer of them than
the search results suggest, and the thing that will actually determine whether you're
happy with a pair is sizing — not the print. Below is what we make, the real measurements,
and what to check before you buy from anyone.

## Why nearly every result is a toddler size

Dinosaur prints sell best in children's sleepwear, so that's where the inventory sits.
Carter's, Old Navy, Gap and Target all rank for this search with kids' pajama pages. Two
things follow from that.

First, if you're shopping for a child, you have enormous choice and you should use it.
Second, if you're shopping for yourself, most of the listings you're scrolling past
simply stop at size 5T, and the few that do go adult are often a "family matching" set
where the adult version is an afterthought.

The upshot: filter for adult sizing before you fall in love with a print. It saves the
disappointment of getting to checkout and finding the largest size is a Youth L.

## Sizing: measure your waist, ignore the letter

This is the part that actually decides whether you keep them. Alpha sizing on print-on-demand
garments does not match the alpha sizing in your wardrobe, and on a drawstring
wide-leg pant you have more room for error at the waist than at the hip. Measure both,
then read the table.

| Size | Waist (in) | Hips (in) |
|---|---|---|
| 2XS | 28 ⅜ | 35 ⅜ |
| XS | 29 ⅞ | 37 |
| S | 31 ½ | 38 ⅝ |
| M | 33 ⅛ | 40 ⅛ |
| L | 36 ¼ | 43 ¼ |
| XL | 39 ⅜ | 46 ½ |
| 2XL | 42 ½ | 49 ⅝ |
| 3XL | 45 ⅝ | 52 ¾ |
| 4XL | 48 ⅞ | 55 ⅞ |
| 5XL | 52 | 59 |
| 6XL | 55 ⅛ | 62 ¼ |

Two notes on reading it. The jump from M to L is about three inches at the waist, which is
larger than the steps below it — if you measure between the two, the drawstring makes
sizing up the safer call. And the range genuinely runs to a 55-inch waist, which is wider
than most dinosaur sleepwear goes; if you've been searching plus size dinosaur pajamas and
finding nothing above a 2XL, that's what the top of this table is for.

## Questions people actually ask

**Are they unisex?** The pants are, genuinely — a relaxed wide-leg cut with a drawstring
waist, sized by waist and hip measurement rather than by gender. The shorts are cut for
women.

**Do you make a one-piece dinosaur onesie?** No. If you specifically want a hooded
one-piece kigurumi, we're not the shop for it. The two-piece approach is deliberate: you
can wear the pants on their own with a t-shirt, which is not true of a costume onesie.

**What's the largest size?** 6XL, which fits a 55 ⅛ inch waist and 62 ¼ inch hip.

**Will the print fade in the wash?** All-over printing puts the ink into the fabric rather
than on top of it, which is why it doesn't crack the way a screen-printed graphic can.
Follow the care label that comes with the garment.

**Are they warm enough to be trousers?** With side pockets and a drawstring, most people
wear these as lounge pants rather than strictly as sleepwear. That's rather the point of
mid-weight jersey.
