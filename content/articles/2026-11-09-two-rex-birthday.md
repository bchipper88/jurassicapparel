# Two Rex Birthday: A Dinosaur Party Plan Built for Two-Year-Olds

Your toddler is turning two, they roar at every dog in the park, and somebody (possibly you) has already said "Two
Rex." Good call. A Two Rex birthday is the easiest dinosaur theme there is, because the pun does half the decorating
for you. The other half is remembering who the party is for: a two-year-old who will be happiest with a short party,
a few friends, some cake and a lot of stomping.

Here's the whole plan, built around what two-year-olds can actually do.

## The Two Rex birthday plan at a glance

| Part | What works at two | Skip it |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 90 minutes, ending well before nap or bedtime | A three-hour afternoon |
| Guests | Roughly as many toddlers as you have grown-ups to watch them | A whole class |
| Games | Three or four, each under 10 minutes, nobody "out" | Anything with winners and losers |
| Cake | One small cake with a big number 2 and a toy dinosaur on top | Elaborate fondant |
| The outfit | A Two Rex shirt, plus matching grown-ups if you like | A full costume they'll refuse to wear |
| Favors | Stickers and a coloring page | Small plastic toys for under-threes |

## Invitations: lean on the pun

Two-year-olds don't read invitations, so these are really for the parents. The words do the work. A few that fit on a
card or a text:

- *"Roar! Our little dino is turning two. Come stomp with us at Ava's Two Rex party."*
- *"T. rex is turning two. Join the herd for cake, games and a lot of roaring."*
- *"It's a Two Rex birthday! Dinosaurs of all sizes welcome."*

Put the end time on the invitation, not just the start. Parents of toddlers plan their whole day around naps, and
"10 to 11:30" tells them this party will fit around theirs.

## Pick the time around the nap, not the other way round

This is the decision that makes or breaks a second birthday. Most two-year-olds still nap once a day, and a party
that runs into it ends in tears, often the birthday kid's. Two slots tend to work:

- **Morning, after breakfast:** for example, 10:00 to 11:30, with cake before lunch-time hunger hits.
- **After the nap:** for example, 3:30 to 5:00, done in time for dinner and bath.

A simple 90-minute running order:

1. **First 20 minutes:** arrivals and free play. Toddlers arrive at different times and need to warm up. Have a
   "dig site" (below) ready so early arrivals have something to do.
2. **Next 30 minutes:** two or three short games, with grown-ups joining in.
3. **Next 20 minutes:** food, then cake and the song.
4. **Last 20 minutes:** more free play, favors and goodbyes. Let people leave when their toddler is done.

## Decorations that take an evening, not a weekend

You don't need a jungle. A few things carry the theme:

- **A big "2" everywhere.** Number balloons, a number on the cake, a number on the door. The Two Rex theme is really
  just "dinosaurs plus the number two," so repeat both.
- **Green and one bright color.** Green plates and balloons plus orange or yellow reads as dinosaur without buying
  anything themed.
- **Dinosaur footprints.** Cut big three-toed footprints from paper and tape them from the front door to the party.
  Two-year-olds love following them, and they'll stomp on every one.
- **A photo spot.** One wall, a few balloons and a "TWO REX" sign. You'll get the photo you actually wanted here,
  usually before the cake, while the outfit is still clean.

## Cake ideas a two-year-old will love

The cake is for the photos and the singing; most two-year-olds eat three bites. Keep it simple:

- **The toy-topper cake:** a plain frosted cake with a big number 2 candle and a washed toy T. rex "eating" the 2.
  Five minutes of decorating, and the toy goes home with the birthday kid.
- **The volcano:** stack a small cake on a big one, frost it brown or green, and pipe orange "lava" down the sides.
- **Dinosaur egg cupcakes:** cupcakes topped with speckled candy eggs in a nest of green frosting. Easy to hand out,
  easy for small hands.

For the food, toddler favorites with dinosaur names go a long way: dino nuggets, "herbivore" fruit and veg cups, and
"lava" ketchup. Cut grapes and cherry tomatoes lengthwise for under-threes.

## Two Rex party games that work at two

Two-year-olds don't do rules, turns or losing. The games that work are short, physical, and something everyone wins.
Here are four that suit this age. Our [dinosaur party games guide](/blogs/blog/dinosaur-party-games) has twenty more,
sorted by age, for when the older cousins show up.

- **Dinosaur stomp:** play music, everyone stomps like a T. rex; when the music stops, everyone freezes and roars.
  Nobody is out. Repeat until they're giggling too hard to freeze.
- **Dig site:** a shallow bin of sand, dry rice or crumpled paper with big plastic dinosaurs buried in it, plus
  scoops and a bucket. This is the best "arrivals" activity there is. Use dinosaurs too big to fit in a mouth.
- **Dinosaur egg hunt:** hide large plastic eggs in plain sight, low down. At two, "hiding" means "on the floor next
  to the couch." Each child gets a bag and keeps what they find.
- **Feed the dinosaur:** a cardboard box painted as a dinosaur with a hole for a mouth, and soft balls or beanbags to
  "feed" it. Stand close. Everyone gets a turn and everyone scores.

Plan three of these and keep the fourth in reserve. Then stop while they still like it.

## Two Rex birthday FAQ

**What is a Two Rex birthday?**
It's a dinosaur-themed second birthday that plays on "T. rex" and "two." The usual elements are a big number 2, a
T. rex on the cake and invitations, and a TWO REX shirt for the birthday kid.

**How long should a two-year-old's birthday party be?**
About 90 minutes. Two-year-olds tire quickly, and most still nap. A short party that ends while everyone is happy beats
a long one that ends in a meltdown.

**How many kids should I invite to a second birthday?**
Fewer than you think. A useful rule is about as many toddlers as you have grown-ups to watch them. Many families
mostly invite relatives and a few friends with kids the same age.

**What games can two-year-olds actually play?**
Short, physical games where everyone wins: freeze-and-roar dancing, a dinosaur dig bin, an easy egg hunt, and tossing
beanbags into a cardboard dinosaur. Skip anything with turns, rules or someone being "out."
