# Dinosaur Party Games: 20 Games Sorted by Age, Plus a Two-Hour Party Plan

You have a living room, a backyard if you're lucky, somewhere between six and sixteen kids, and about two hours before
the sugar takes over. These dinosaur party games are sorted by age and by how much space they need, and each one
lists what you need to have ready. The plan at the bottom puts them in an order that works.

Here's the one to start with, because it works at every age from two to ten:

**T. rex Tag.** It's ordinary tag with one rule: whoever is "it" is a T. rex, so their elbows stay glued to their ribs and
they can only tag with their fingertips. Those tiny arms slow the tagger down just enough that little kids can escape
and big kids have to work for it. When someone gets tagged they roar and they're the new T. rex. You don't need any
supplies, only a space with no coffee table in it.

## Dinosaur party games for toddlers (ages 2–3)

Two-year-olds don't take turns well, they don't do rules well, and they don't do losing at all. Pick games where everyone
moves at once and everyone gets something. Plan on about ten minutes a game and have a grown-up in the middle of it.

**2. Dino Says.** It's Simon Says without anyone getting knocked out. The caller says *"Dino says stomp like a T. rex,"*
*"Dino says stretch your neck like a Brachiosaurus,"* *"Dino says flap like a Pteranodon."* Pteranodon was a flying
reptile, not a dinosaur, and the five-year-olds in the room will be thrilled to tell you so. When you call one without
"Dino says," the kids who moved just have to roar, and then everyone keeps playing.

**3. Hatch the Egg.** The kids curl up on the floor under a big blanket (that's the egg). A grown-up taps the shell
three times and counts down, and everyone bursts out roaring as a brand-new baby dinosaur. Toddlers will want to do it
again and again. Let them, and stop while they're still laughing.

**4. Feed the Dinosaur.** Cut a big mouth in the side of a cardboard box, draw teeth around it, and let the kids throw
bean bags or balled-up socks into it from a taped line on the floor. You can move the line closer for the littlest
ones. Nobody keeps score.

**5. Dinosaur Egg Hunt.** Fill plastic eggs, hide them in plain sight for toddlers and in trickier spots for older kids,
and give every child a basket or paper bag. Decide the rule before you start: *each kid finds five, then helps a friend*.
That way one fast four-year-old doesn't clear the whole yard. Put a dinosaur sticker in each egg instead of candy (see
prizes below). Set aside a few eggs to quietly drop in front of anyone who's coming up short.

## Games for preschoolers (ages 3–5)

At this age kids can follow a rule and wait about one turn. Keep teams out of it and keep any winning low-stakes.

**6. Fossil Dig.** This is the one kids talk about afterwards. The day before, make "bones" out of salt dough (2 cups
flour, 1 cup salt, about ¾ cup water, shaped into bones and baked low until hard). Bury them in a sandbox, a plastic
storage tub of sand, or a kiddie pool of dry rice. Give each kid a paintbrush and a spoon, and tell them real
paleontologists use brushes because digging fast breaks fossils. Then watch how carefully a four-year-old will brush.
If you get two minutes of quiet in the middle of the party, it'll probably be here. Salt dough isn't food and it's very
salty, so keep an eye on toddlers (and the dog).

**7. Pin the Tail on the Dinosaur.** Draw a big dinosaur on poster board or tape together a printed outline. Use paper
tails with tape loops on the back. Blindfolds scare some three-year-olds, so let them close their eyes instead.
Finding the closest tail is half the fun, so leave every tail where it landed.

**8. Hot Lava Crossing.** Lay couch cushions, pillows or paper plates across the floor as stepping stones over the
"lava." Kids cross one at a time from the volcano to the safe nest. After every round, take one stone away. If someone
steps in the lava, they go back to the start; nobody's out.

**9. Nest Swap.** This is musical chairs with hula hoops or pillows as dinosaur nests, and with one important change: when
the music stops, you take a nest away but nobody leaves. Kids squeeze into fewer and fewer nests until the whole party is
piled into the last one, giggling. It's the same tension as musical chairs, with nobody crying in the corner.

**20. Raptor Sneak.** It's Red Light, Green Light as a hunt. One kid is the sleeping T. rex at the far end of the room
with their back turned. Everyone else is a raptor sneaking up. When the T. rex spins around and roars, the raptors have
to freeze. Anyone caught moving goes back to the start, and the first raptor to touch the T. rex's tail (a scarf tucked
in a waistband) becomes the next T. rex.

## Games for kids 5–8

Now kids can handle teams, relays and actual winning, though they'll still want it to be fair. Those games also run
faster and louder, so this is the age where you need a yard or a park.

**10. Dinosaur Egg-and-Spoon Race.** Use plastic eggs for the younger ones and hard-boiled eggs if you're brave. Kids
walk the course balancing an egg on a spoon, and if it drops they stop and put it back. For a relay, they hand the
spoon to the next teammate.

**11. Dino Walk Relay.** Every leg of the relay is a different dinosaur walk. Leg one is a Velociraptor sprint with hands
tucked up as claws. Leg two is a Stegosaurus crawl on all fours. Leg three is a Brachiosaurus walk on tiptoes with arms
stretched up as a long neck. Leg four is a T. rex stomp with tiny arms and big steps. Kids will spend more time laughing
at each other than racing, and that's fine.

**12. Meteor Dodge.** Two or three grown-ups or older kids gently roll soft foam balls (the meteors) across a marked
area while the dinosaurs dodge them. Roll the balls along the ground, don't throw them. A dinosaur who gets hit becomes
a meteor-roller, and the last dinosaur standing survives the extinction.

**13. Paleontologist Scavenger Hunt.** Write 8–10 clue cards that lead from one spot to the next (*"Where do dinosaurs
keep their snacks cold?"* sends them to the fridge). At the end there's a "fossil" treasure: the prize bag, or a box of
the salt-dough bones from the dig. For kids who can't read yet, draw picture clues. Hand the clue cards out in turns so
one confident reader doesn't run the whole hunt.

**14. Guess My Dinosaur.** One kid wears a headband (or holds a card on their forehead) with a dinosaur name and picture
they can't see. They ask yes-or-no questions to figure it out: *"Did I eat meat?" "Did I have horns?" "Was I taller
than a house?"* Five-year-olds will need picture cards of the big-name dinosaurs. Eight-year-olds can handle
Parasaurolophus.

**15. Volcano Race.** Set up two baking-soda volcanoes, one per team: a plastic bottle in a mound of play dough or sand,
with 2–3 tablespoons of baking soda inside. Each team runs a relay, and when their last runner gets back they pour in
the vinegar (add red food coloring and a squirt of dish soap for better lava). The first team to erupt wins. Do it
outside; vinegar foam goes everywhere.

## Games for older kids and mixed ages (7–12)

Older kids at a dinosaur party usually know a lot of dinosaur facts and are very sure they're right. These games put
that knowledge to use.

**16. Dinosaur Line-Up.** Make one card per dinosaur. Teams race to put themselves in order from shortest to longest. The
approximate lengths, so you can be the judge:

| Dinosaur | Approx. length |
|---|---|
| Microraptor | 2.5 ft (0.8 m) |
| Velociraptor | 6.5 ft (2 m) |
| Pachycephalosaurus | 15 ft (4.5 m) |
| Triceratops | 29 ft (9 m) |
| Tyrannosaurus rex | 40 ft (12 m) |
| Brachiosaurus | 85 ft (26 m) |
| Argentinosaurus | about 110 ft (around 35 m) |

Most kids put Velociraptor too high, because the movie raptors were much bigger than the real animal, which was about
turkey-sized. Do the line-up a second time sorted by when each dinosaur lived and it gets even harder.

**17. Dinosaur Trivia Showdown.** Split into two teams and give each team a buzzer (a squeaky toy works fine). Read
questions from easy to hard. Our [dinosaur trivia questions](/blogs/blog/dinosaur-trivia) are already sorted by
difficulty and have the answers, so you can run it straight off your phone. Let the youngest kid on each team answer the
easy ones.

**18. Asteroid!** This one is Four Corners. Label the four corners of the room *Triassic*, *Jurassic*, *Cretaceous* and
*Ice Age*. One kid stands in the middle with eyes closed and counts to ten while everyone else tiptoes to a corner.
Then the counter shouts "Asteroid!" and names a corner, and everyone standing there is extinct and sits out to help
count the next round. It's the one elimination game on this list, and it works because rounds take about 30 seconds.
Nobody sits out for long. (The Ice Age corner is the joke: the dinosaurs that weren't birds had been gone for more than 60
million years by then, and someone will point it out.)

**19. Dinosaur Bingo.** Make bingo cards with dinosaur pictures or names in the squares, in a different order on each
card. Call from a hat. Little kids can mark squares with stickers instead of a pen, so the game doubles as a craft. Older
kids can play "fact bingo," where you read a clue (*"I had three horns"*) and they have to work out which square it is.

## How to run dinosaur birthday party games without tears

- **Plan five or six games for two hours and expect to play four.** Arrivals, food and cake take more time than you think.
- **Alternate loud and quiet.** Put a calm game (Fossil Dig, Bingo, Pin the Tail) after every running game. The kids
  settle down and so do you.
- **Keep the eliminations short.** Getting knocked out of the first round of a 15-minute game is how a party turns.
  Use the no-elimination versions above for anyone under six.
- **Explain every game in under 30 seconds.** Show them, don't tell them. Play one practice round.
- **Give the birthday kid one job,** like caller in Dino Says or the first T. rex. Don't make them win everything; the
  other kids notice.
- **Have one free station going the whole time.** A table with crayons and dinosaur coloring pages catches early
  arrivals, kids who don't want to run, and the one who needs a break. Our

## A two-hour dinosaur party running order

Copy this and swap in the games you picked.

| Time | What happens | Games |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Arrivals | Coloring table; Dino Says for whoever's there |
| 0:15–0:35 | First big game | Egg Hunt (2–5) or Paleontologist Scavenger Hunt (5–10) |
| 0:35–0:50 | Burn off energy | T. rex Tag, Hot Lava or Meteor Dodge |
| 0:50–1:05 | Calm down | Fossil Dig, Pin the Tail or Dinosaur Bingo |
| 1:05–1:25 | Food | Guess My Dinosaur at the table for older kids |
| 1:25–1:40 | Cake and song | — |
| 1:40–1:52 | One last big game | Volcano Race outside, or Nest Swap inside |
| 1:52–2:00 | Prizes and goodbyes | Hand out stickers at the door |

Putting the biggest game right after cake is deliberate. Kids are about to burn through that sugar anyway, and an
eruption is a good ending.

## FAQ

**How many games do I need for a two-hour dinosaur party?**
Plan five or six games and a coloring table that stays open the whole time. You'll realistically play four. Arrivals,
food and cake take up nearly an hour.

**What are good dinosaur party games for 3-year-olds?**
Pick games where everyone plays at once and nobody gets knocked out: Dino Says (no-elimination Simon Says), Hatch the
Egg, Feed the Dinosaur and a small egg hunt with a "find five" rule. Keep each one to about ten minutes.

**What dinosaur games work indoors in a small space?**
Dino Says, Hatch the Egg, Feed the Dinosaur, Pin the Tail, Nest Swap, Guess My Dinosaur, Dinosaur Bingo and the Line-Up
all fit in a living room. For T. rex Tag indoors, make the rule walking only.

**How do I run games for mixed ages?**
Choose games where the age gap helps instead of hurts: T. rex Tag (little kids are hard to catch), Raptor Sneak, and
trivia teams where the youngest answers the easy questions. For races, give the older kids a handicap, like hopping
the Velociraptor leg on one foot.

**What can I use instead of candy in the egg hunt?**
Use stickers, tiny erasers, a "coupon" for being the first T. rex in tag, or a salt-dough fossil. Stickers fit in the
smallest plastic eggs and every kid can get the same number.

**What else do I need to plan besides games?**
Food, decorations, invitations and a cake that looks like a dinosaur. Our
[dinosaur birthday party guide](/blogs/dinosaur-birthday-party/the-ultimate-guide-to-throwing-an-unforgettable-dinosaur-birthday-party)
covers the rest of the party. Use this page for the games.
