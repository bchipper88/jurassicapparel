# Dinosaur Pick Up Lines: 80+ Cheesy, Nerdy and Sweet Lines (and How to Land Them)

Nobody has ever been won over by a dinosaur pick up line because it was smooth. They work because
they're terrible, you know they're terrible, and you said it anyway. That takes nerve, and nerve
is attractive. So these lines are sorted by where you'll use them: a bar, a first-date text, a
partner who already puts up with you, or a classroom Valentine for a seven-year-old.

We sell dinosaur shirts, mugs and gifts for a living, so we spend a lot of time on dinosaur wordplay.
We kept the lines that made us laugh, or at least groan, and cut the near-repeats.

## The ten best dinosaur pick up lines

If you only need one, use one of these.

1. Are you a T. rex? Because I can't keep my tiny hands off you.
2. I'd go extinct for you. Again.
3. You must be a fossil, because I've been digging you for millions of years.
4. Are you a meteor? Because when I saw you, my whole world ended.
5. I don't need a time machine. Being with you already feels prehistoric. In a good way.
6. You're the Tri to my ceratops.
7. If I were a dinosaur, I'd be a Pterodacty-love.
8. Are you a paleontologist? Because I'm all bones when I'm around you.
9. My love for you is like a brachiosaurus neck: long, a little ridiculous, and it keeps going.
10. You make my heart go *rawr*.

## Nerdy dinosaur pick up lines for science people

For the museum date, the geology major, or anyone who'll correct you if you get a fact wrong.
Every fact in these is true, which is part of the joke.

- I'd carbon-date you, but carbon dating only works back about 50,000 years. You need something more serious.
- Stegosaurus and T. rex were separated by more time than T. rex and us. We've got no excuse for being apart.
- Birds are dinosaurs, so technically every pigeon in this park is cheering for us.
- Are you a fossil? Because you look like you were preserved in perfect conditions.
- You must be a layer of sediment, because I want to settle down with you.
- I wish you were an index fossil, so I'd know exactly when we met.
- You're my K-Pg boundary. Everything before you looks different from everything after.
- Are you amber? Because I'd like to be stuck with you for a very long time.
- I'm like a coprolite. Unattractive at first, but fascinating once you get to know me.
- You're more than a stratum to me. You're my whole formation.
- If you were a dinosaur, you'd be a Dreadnoughtus, because I'm dreading the moment you leave.
- Bones turn to stone over millions of years. My feelings for you took about four minutes.

That carbon-dating line is the one to keep. It works on anyone who knows the fact, and it's a good
way to teach it to anyone who doesn't.

## How to land a dinosaur pick up line

The line itself matters less than how you say it. A few things help:

- **Commit.** The worst delivery is the apologetic one. Say it as if you think it's the best line
  ever written. The confidence is the joke.
- **Know your audience.** The nerdy lines work on people who already like dinosaurs. The cheesy ones
  work on everyone, because they're about the cheese, not the dinosaurs.
- **Have a follow-up.** If they laugh, you need something real to say next. "I'm sorry, that was
  terrible. I'm [name]" works fine.
- **Never use two in a row.** One is charming. Two is a bit.

## Dinosaur pick up lines FAQ

**What's the best dinosaur pick up line?**
"Are you a T. rex? Because I can't keep my tiny hands off you." It's short, everyone knows the joke
about T. rex arms, and it sounds just confident enough.

**Are there dinosaur pick up lines for kids?**
Yes, but for kids they're friendship lines rather than pick up lines. Use the classroom Valentines
section above. "You're dino-mite!" and "I'd never go extinct on you" are short enough for the smallest card.

**What do you write on a dinosaur Valentine?**
One line and a name. "You're roar-some, love Sam" fits on the smallest card and reads well in a
seven-year-old's handwriting.

**What's a nerdy dinosaur pick up line that's actually accurate?**
"Stegosaurus and T. rex were separated by more time than T. rex and us." It's true: Stegosaurus
lived about 150 million years ago and T. rex about 68 to 66 million years ago, so roughly 80
million years separate them, against 66 million between T. rex and us.

**Does "rawr" really mean "I love you" in dinosaur?**
It's an internet joke, not paleontology. Nobody knows what dinosaurs sounded like, and some
scientists think they made closed-mouth booming sounds, more like an ostrich than a roar. It
works on a card anyway.
