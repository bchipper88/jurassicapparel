# Dinosaur First Birthday: A One-A-Saurus Party Plan That Works for a One-Year-Old

Your baby is about to turn one. They won't remember the party, but you will, and so will the photos. A dinosaur first
birthday is a lovely theme for that: "One-A-Saurus" is a pun the grown-ups will smile at, the colors are easy, and a
baby T. rex in a party hat is about as sweet as birthday art gets.

The trick is planning for who the guest of honor actually is. A one-year-old can't play party games, won't sit through
presents and will need a nap at some point. So this plan is built around three things that do work at one: a short
party, a smash cake, and good pictures.

## The dinosaur first birthday at a glance

| Part | What works at one | Skip it |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 60–90 minutes, timed between naps | An all-afternoon party |
| Guests | Family and a few close friends; other babies are optional | Inviting the whole playgroup |
| Activities | A cake smash, a photo spot, a play mat with soft toys | Organized games |
| Cake | A small smash cake for the baby, a separate cake for guests | One big cake everyone eats after the smash |
| Outfit | A bodysuit that stays put while they crawl and smash | Anything with buttons, tulle or a long hem |
| Food | Soft, cut-up finger food the baby can share | Whole grapes, nuts, popcorn |
| Favors | Something for older sibling guests, or skip them | Small toys for under-threes |

## One-A-Saurus invitation wording

Keep it short and put the end time on the invitation. Guests with babies of their own will thank you, and it saves you
from a party that drifts into nap time.

A few lines to borrow:

- *"Our little dino is hatching into a ONE-A-SAURUS! Stomp over to celebrate [Name]'s first birthday."*
- *"Roar! [Name] is turning one. Join us for cake, a little smashing and a lot of photos."*
- *"Something big is hatching: [Name]'s first trip around the sun. Dinosaurs welcome."*

Add the date, the start and end time, the address, and one line on parking or shoes off if that matters at your house.
If you're asking guests not to bring gifts, say it plainly here.

## A schedule built around naps

The best time for a first birthday is right after the baby wakes up, fed and happy. For most one-year-olds that means a
late-morning party after the morning nap, or a mid-afternoon one after the midday nap. Here's a 75-minute running
order:

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Guests arrive. The baby is up from a nap and fed, in their outfit. Snacks are out. |
| 0:10 | Photo spot open: the backdrop, the number 1, a few dinosaurs. Get the good photos now, while they're clean. |
| 0:30 | Food for everyone. Put the baby in the high chair with some finger food. |
| 0:45 | Happy birthday song, then the smash cake. Give it ten minutes; some babies dive in, some just poke it. |
| 0:55 | Clean-up for the baby (and the high chair). A fresh outfit if you need one. |
| 1:00 | Guest cake, coffee, presents opened later or not at all. |
| 1:15 | Thank everyone and wave them off before the next nap. |

If the baby gets overwhelmed, take them to a quiet room for ten minutes. That's normal at one, and the party will
carry on without them.

## Dinosaur first birthday decorations

You don't need much. A one-year-old is most interested in balloons, faces and whatever's on the floor, so put the
effort where the photos are.

- **A color scheme of two or three colors.** Green and orange is the classic. Sage, cream and terracotta looks softer
  in photos. Pastel blue or pink with green works too.
- **One photo backdrop.** A plain wall with a balloon garland, a big "1" and a few large paper palm leaves is enough.
  Set it at the baby's height, so you can sit them in front of it.
- **Dinosaurs on the table.** Soft dinosaur toys or plush make good centerpieces, and the baby can actually hold them.
  Avoid small plastic figures within reach of anyone under three.
- **A high-chair banner.** A strip of "ONE" or "1" bunting across the high chair is the photo you'll keep.
- **Dinosaur "eggs."** Wrap a few balloons or round paper lanterns in a speckled pattern and group them in a nest of
  tissue paper. It's the easiest dinosaur prop there is.

Lay down a washable mat or an old sheet under the high chair before the cake comes out. You'll be glad you did.

## The smash cake

The smash cake is the main event of a first birthday, and it's the photo most families plan around. Some ideas that
keep it simple:

- **A small round cake** (around 4 to 6 inches) in a soft green, with a single edible decoration: a fondant egg,
  a few chocolate "footprints," or nothing at all.
- **A "dinosaur egg" cake:** a small dome cake frosted in pale blue or cream with brown speckles. It photographs well
  and needs no piping skills.
- **A volcano cake** for the guests: a tall cake with orange and red frosting "lava" running down the sides. Keep the
  baby's smash cake separate and plain.

Many parents make the baby's cake lower in sugar, using banana or applesauce, with a light frosting such as whipped cream
or a cream-cheese frosting. Ask your pediatrician if you're unsure about any ingredient. Take toppers, candles and picks
off the smash cake before it goes in front of the baby.

**For the photos:** put the baby in the outfit, use daylight from a window rather than a flash, and shoot from the
baby's eye level. Have a second person ready with a cloth.

## Food for a first birthday party

Serve one table that works for babies and grown-ups alike, so nobody has to cook twice.

- **Baby-friendly:** soft-cooked veggie sticks, banana slices, avocado, cheese cubes, soft pasta, berries cut small, and
  grapes quartered lengthwise.
- **For the grown-ups:** a "dino bites" platter of sandwiches cut with a dinosaur cookie cutter, fruit kebabs, a dip
  with vegetables, and "fossil" cookies pressed with a toy dinosaur's footprint.
- **Leave out:** whole grapes, whole nuts, popcorn and hard sweets on any table a toddler can reach.

Label the platters with little cards ("Brachio Bites," "Herbivore Greens," "T. rex Treats"). It's the cheapest
decoration on the list.

## Favors, and what to skip

At a first birthday, favors are really for the other kids who come. Babies under three shouldn't be given small toys, so
skip the tiny plastic dinosaurs.

## Dinosaur first birthday FAQ

**What is a One-A-Saurus party?**
It's a dinosaur first birthday. "One-A-Saurus" is a play on dinosaur names, the same way "Two Rex" works for a second
birthday and "Three Rex" for a third.

**How long should a first birthday party be?**
About 60 to 90 minutes. A one-year-old will be tired after that, and a short party ends on a happy baby instead of a
crying one.

**Do I need games at a first birthday?**
No. One-year-olds can't play organized games. A play mat with soft toys and the cake smash are plenty. If older children
are coming, set out coloring pages or a simple dinosaur dig in a tray.
