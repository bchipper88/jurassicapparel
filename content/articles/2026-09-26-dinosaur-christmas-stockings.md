# Dinosaur Christmas Stockings: 15 Designs, One Size, and When to Order

Somebody in your house has asked for a dinosaur stocking. Maybe it's a four-year-old who
won't hang anything else on the mantel, maybe it's the adult who has had a T. rex on every
birthday cake since 1993. Either way, you want to know what's out there, whether it's big
enough to be worth filling, and whether it will turn up before Christmas Eve.

That last question matters more than usual here, so we'll be straight about it up front:
our stockings are printed to order and the shipping estimate is long. Everything you need
to decide is below — the fifteen designs, the real size, the fabric, and the dates.

## What every stocking has in common

All fifteen designs are the same stocking underneath. Only the print changes.

- **Size:** one size, 13″ × 19.3″ (33 × 49 cm). That's a full-size stocking, not a mini
  ornament version.
- **Fabric:** 100% polyester faux suede, which has a soft, slightly brushed feel rather
  than the stiff felt of a supermarket stocking.
- **Hanging:** a loop at the cuff, so it goes on a mantel hook, a stair rail, a door knob
  or a bedpost.
- **Price:** $34.99 for fourteen of the designs; the Merry Christmas print is $33.55.

One thing they are not: personalized. There is no name field on these listings, so what
you see in the photo is what arrives.

## Picking a dinosaur stocking for each person

The trick most families use to tell stockings apart is a name across the cuff. With fifteen
prints to choose from, you can do it with the design instead — everyone gets their own
dinosaur, and nobody argues over whose is whose. Here is how the range sorts out.

### For small children: bright and busy

- **[Cute Dino Christmas](/products/cute-dino-christmas-dinosaur-stocking)** — a royal
  blue background crowded with pink, green and turquoise dinosaurs and wrapped presents.
  The loudest of the fifteen, and the one most likely to be spotted from across the room
  at 5 a.m.
- **Festive Celebration** — yellow and green cartoon dinosaurs in scarves on white, with
  cookies and gifts scattered between them.
- **Dino Christmas Party** — pastel dinosaurs in Christmas sweaters under strings of
  bunting, on white.
- **Christmas Nuggies** — orange dino-nugget shapes in little Santa hats. For the child
  whose favorite dinner is also shaped like a dinosaur.
- **Cookies & Trees** — lime-green dinosaurs and trees on white; the lightest and
  airiest of the patterned ones.

### For a traditional mantel: darker grounds

If the stockings hang next to garland and a wreath, the dark-background prints sit better
than the white ones.

- **[Christmas Cookies](/products/christmas-cookies-dinosaur-christmas-stocking)** —
  gingerbread-colored dinosaurs, trees and hearts on navy. It reads as a classic cookie
  print until you look closely.
- **Christmas Party** — dinosaurs and presents on deep forest green.
- **Merry Christmas Night** — chocolate brown with crescent moons, snowmen, trees and
  dinosaurs pulling sleighs.
- **Merry Christmas** — dark green dinosaurs on Christmas red, with "Merry Christmas"
  lettering worked into the pattern.

### For something softer

- **Winter Wonderland (pink)** — dusty pink with pastel dinosaurs and trees. The only
  stocking in the range that isn't red, green or white at heart.
- **Winter Wonderland (white)** — green dinosaurs in Santa hats between Christmas trees
  and candy canes. Two different listings share the Winter Wonderland name; the photos
  tell them apart.
- **Dinos & Snow Globes** — cream, with green trees and little dinosaurs inside snow
  globes. The quietest print of the fifteen.

### One big dinosaur instead of a pattern

- **T-Rex Gingerbread** — a single huge T. rex in a Santa hat, wrapped in Christmas
  lights, filling the whole leg on a sky-blue background.
- **Dino Holly** — T. rex heads in Santa hats, each framed in holly, repeated down the
  stocking on white.
- **[Drinking Rex](/products/drinking-rex-dinosaur-christmas-stocking)** — a T. rex in a
  Santa suit raising a beer, with "Merry Christmas" in gold script, on black. This is the
  grown-up one. Probably not for the kids' side of the fireplace.

## When to order a dinosaur Christmas stocking

Here's the part to read twice. Each stocking is made when you order it, and the listings
give two separate estimates for US standard shipping:

| Stage | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Production | 2–5 business days |
| Shipping | 10–30 business days |
| **Door to door** | **12–35 business days** |

Thirty-five business days is seven working weeks. That is much longer than our pajamas or
the [dinosaur ornaments](/blogs/blog/dinosaur-christmas-ornaments-guide), and it's the
reason this guide is going up in September. Counting back from the listings' own
estimates, skipping weekends and Thanksgiving:

| If you want it… | Order by (slowest estimate) |
|---|---|
| Hanging for Thanksgiving weekend (Nov 27) | **October 8** |
| Up for the first weekend of December (Dec 5) | **October 16** |
| Filled by Christmas Eve (Dec 23 arrival) | **November 3** |

At the middle of the estimate, a stocking ordered around November 19 would make it by
Christmas; at the fastest, one ordered on December 7 would. But those are the good cases,
not the promise. If a stocking arriving on December 28 would genuinely
ruin something, order before November. Tracking is sent once your order moves into the
shipping stage. These stockings ship within the US only.

## Questions people ask

**How big are the stockings?** 13″ × 19.3″ (33 × 49 cm). There's only one size.

**Can I get a name put on one?** No. None of the fifteen has a personalization option.
Families usually use the different prints to tell them apart instead.

**What are they made of?** 100% polyester faux suede, with a hanging loop.

**Are they sturdy enough to hold a heavy present?** They're fabric stockings with a fabric
loop, so they're made for the usual small things — candy, socks, small toys. For anything
heavy, check what your mantel hook is rated for before you trust it.

**Do you ship outside the US?** Not for these. The stocking listings offer US standard
shipping only.

**Will they arrive by Christmas?** If you order by November 3, the listings' slowest
estimate still gets it to you by December 23. After that it becomes a gamble, and after
early December even the fastest estimate doesn't make it.
