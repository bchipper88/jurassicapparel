# Dinosaur Riddles: 58 “What Am I?” Riddles for Kids (With Answers)

A joke gives you the punchline. A riddle makes you work for it, and that's why kids who have
heard every dinosaur joke still want more dinosaur riddles. Below are 58 of them, sorted from
"my three-year-old got it" to "my brother-in-law didn't." Every answer is on the page, and every
clue is true, so a kid who solves these learns something about real dinosaurs without noticing.

If you're reading aloud, cover the answer column with your hand or a sticky note.

## The ten best dinosaur riddles

Short on time? Start here. These work for almost any age.

| Riddle | Answer |
|---|---|
| I have three horns and a frill, but I'm not wearing a party hat. | Triceratops |
| I had plates on my back, spikes on my tail, and a very small brain for my size. | Stegosaurus |
| My neck was so long I could eat from the treetops without standing on tiptoe. | Brachiosaurus |
| I had armor like a tank and a club at the end of my tail. | Ankylosaurus |
| I'm a dinosaur that's alive today, and I wake the farm up every morning. | A rooster. Birds are dinosaurs. |
| What came first, the dinosaur or the egg? | The egg. Reptiles were laying eggs long before the first dinosaur hatched. |
| I had a long tube-shaped crest on my head, and scientists think I could honk through it. | Parasaurolophus |
| I'm big, I'm scary, and my arms are too short to clap. | Tyrannosaurus rex |
| How many dinosaurs can you put in an empty box? | One. After that, the box isn't empty. |
| I'm made of stone, but I used to be alive. | A fossil |

## Easy dinosaur riddles for little kids (ages 3–5)

Keep it to one or two clues and let them shout the answer. At this age, "a dinosaur!" is a
perfectly good guess, so give a hint about shape or sound instead of a fact.

| Riddle | Answer |
|---|---|
| I go ROAR, I have big sharp teeth, and I'm the king. | T. rex |
| I have a long, long neck and a long, long tail, and I eat leaves all day. | A long-necked dinosaur, like Diplodocus |
| I'm round and hard, and there's a baby dinosaur inside me. | A dinosaur egg |
| I just came out of my egg. I'm a tiny dinosaur. | A baby dinosaur (scientists say "hatchling") |
| I flew in the sky when dinosaurs were alive, and my wings were made of skin. | A pterosaur, like Pteranodon |
| I have spikes on my tail and big plates on my back. | Stegosaurus |
| My mouth was flat and wide, a bit like a duck's. | A duck-billed dinosaur |
| I have a sail on my back, but I'm not a boat. | Spinosaurus |
| I'm soft, I'm squishy, and I sleep on your bed. | A stuffed dinosaur |
| People come to the museum to look at my bones. | A dinosaur skeleton |
| I'm soft and bristly, and I dust the dirt off fossils. | A brush |
| My job is digging up dinosaur bones. | A paleontologist |

The long word at the end is the payoff. Most four-year-olds love being able to say
"paleontologist," and it's a good one to save for last.

## "What am I?" dinosaur riddles (ages 6–9)

These are three-clue riddles, and they're the heart of the game. Read clue one. If no one gets
it, read clue two, then clue three. The first two clues are hard on purpose; the third one nearly
gives it away.

Score it like this: solved on clue one is 3 points, clue two is 2 points, clue three is 1 point.
That keeps the fast kid from winning every round, because a slower guess still scores.

1. **Clue 1:** My name means "egg thief." **Clue 2:** That name was a mistake: I was probably
   sitting on my own eggs. **Clue 3:** I had a beak and no teeth.
   **Answer:** Oviraptor
2. **Clue 1:** My claws were longer than your arm. **Clue 2:** I probably used them to pull down
   branches, because I mostly ate plants. **Clue 3:** My name starts with "Therizino."
   **Answer:** Therizinosaurus
3. **Clue 1:** I was about the size of a crow. **Clue 2:** I had long feathers on my arms and my
   legs, so I had four wings. **Clue 3:** My name has "raptor" in it, and "micro" means small.
   **Answer:** Microraptor
4. **Clue 1:** My name means "good mother lizard." **Clue 2:** I built nests in big groups, like a
   colony of birds. **Clue 3:** My fossils were found in Montana.
   **Answer:** Maiasaura
5. **Clue 1:** I had two horns above my eyes. **Clue 2:** My arms were even tinier than a T. rex's.
   **Clue 3:** My name means "meat-eating bull."
   **Answer:** Carnotaurus
6. **Clue 1:** I had a spike on each thumb. **Clue 2:** Scientists once put that spike on my nose
   by mistake. **Clue 3:** I was one of the first dinosaurs ever named, back in 1825.
   **Answer:** Iguanodon
7. **Clue 1:** I'm one of the biggest animals that ever walked on land. **Clue 2:** I lived in
   South America. **Clue 3:** My name starts with the name of a country.
   **Answer:** Argentinosaurus
8. **Clue 1:** I had a horn on my nose. **Clue 2:** I hunted in the Jurassic, millions of years
   before T. rex. **Clue 3:** My name means "horned lizard."
   **Answer:** Ceratosaurus
9. **Clue 1:** I had feathers and wings, but also teeth and a long bony tail. **Clue 2:** My
   fossils were found in Germany. **Clue 3:** I'm famous for being half dinosaur, half bird.
   **Answer:** Archaeopteryx
10. **Clue 1:** I lived in the sea. **Clue 2:** I had four flippers and, often, a very long neck.
    **Clue 3:** People call me a dinosaur, but I'm a different kind of reptile.
    **Answer:** A plesiosaur
11. **Clue 1:** I had one big curved claw on each foot. **Clue 2:** I lived in North America.
    **Clue 3:** The movie raptors look more like me than like my smaller cousin Velociraptor.
    **Answer:** Deinonychus
12. **Clue 1:** I had a tall sail on my back. **Clue 2:** I'm older than every dinosaur.
    **Clue 3:** I'm more closely related to you than to T. rex.
    **Answer:** Dimetrodon

Number 12 catches almost everyone. Dimetrodon shows up in most toy dinosaur sets, but it lived
before the dinosaurs and belongs to the group that eventually led to mammals.

## Hard dinosaur riddles for older kids and adults

These need a real fact to solve. They work well as a tiebreaker or for the grown-ups at the
party who think dinosaur riddles are for kids.

| Riddle | Answer |
|---|---|
| I lived closer in time to you than to Stegosaurus. Who am I? | T. rex. Stegosaurus died out around 80 million years before T. rex appeared. |
| I'm fossilized, I'm useful to scientists, and I'm poop. | A coprolite |
| I'm a crater about 110 miles wide, buried near the coast of Mexico. | Chicxulub, where the asteroid hit 66 million years ago |
| I'm a word invented in 1842, and I mean "terrible lizard." | "Dinosaur" |
| In 1824 I became the first dinosaur to get a scientific name. | Megalosaurus |
| Three periods, one era. Name the era. | The Mesozoic (Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous) |
| I'm a T. rex named after the woman who found me, and I live in Chicago. | Sue, at the Field Museum |
| I ruled the dinosaurs' seas and grew longer than a school bus, but I was a giant lizard, not a dinosaur. | Mosasaurus |
| I'm older than the dinosaurs, I'm still in the ocean today, and I have rows of teeth. | A shark |
| My name means "thunder lizard," and scientists argued for a century about whether I was real. | Brontosaurus. A 2015 study brought the name back. |

## Trick dinosaur riddles

These play with words instead of facts, and half the answers aren't dinosaurs. Save them for when everyone thinks
they've figured out how riddles work.

| Riddle | Answer |
|---|---|
| What do dinosaurs have that no other animal has? | Baby dinosaurs |
| What's as big as a Brachiosaurus but weighs nothing? | Its shadow |
| What has a spine and is full of dinosaurs, but has no bones? | A dinosaur book |
| The more fossils you take out of me, the bigger I get. | A hole |
| Which dinosaur can jump higher than a house? | All of them. Houses can't jump. |
| What starts with T, ends with X, and couldn't reach its own nose? | T. rex |
| Two baby dinosaurs hatch from the same nest at the same minute, but they aren't twins. How? | They're two of a bigger clutch. Most dinosaurs laid several eggs at a time. |
| I'm always running, but I've never had legs, and dinosaurs drank from me. | A river |

## Fossil and dig-site riddles

Good for a museum trip, a backyard dig, or a class that's just started a fossils unit.

| Riddle | Answer |
|---|---|
| I'm golden and I used to be sticky, and I can hold a bug for millions of years. | Amber |
| I'm not a bone, but I prove a dinosaur walked right here. | A trace fossil, like a footprint |
| The deeper you dig into me, the older the things you find. | Rock layers (scientists call them strata) |
| I'm a bone that turned to stone because minerals filled in all my tiny holes. | A fossil bone |
| I'm a white jacket, but nobody wears me. Fossil hunters wrap bones in me to carry them home. | A plaster jacket |
| I found giant sea-reptile fossils on an English beach more than 200 years ago. | Mary Anning |

## How to write your own dinosaur riddles

Kids who've solved a few will want to write their own, and it's a sneaky bit of research. Here's
the formula behind every three-clue riddle above:

1. **Clue one is a surprising fact.** Something hard to guess: where it lived, what its name
   means, a mistake scientists made.
2. **Clue two narrows it down.** A body part or a size.
3. **Clue three nearly gives it away.** The first letter, a famous feature, or what it rhymes with.

Have them pick a dinosaur, find three facts in a book or in our
[dinosaur trivia](/blogs/blog/dinosaur-trivia) list, and put them in order from hardest to easiest.
Then they test it on someone. If the grown-up gets it on clue one, the clues need to be harder.

## Dinosaur riddles FAQ

**What's the difference between a dinosaur riddle and a dinosaur joke?**
A joke asks a question so it can deliver a punchline ("What do you call a sleeping dinosaur?").
A riddle gives clues and expects you to work out the answer. If you want the punchlines, we have
90+ of them in our [dinosaur jokes](/blogs/blog/dinosaur-jokes) post.

**What are good dinosaur riddles for preschoolers?**
One-clue riddles about shape or sound: "I go ROAR and I have big teeth," "I have spikes on my
tail," "I'm round and there's a baby inside me." Accept close guesses and give the real name
as the answer.

**What's a hard dinosaur riddle?**
"I lived closer in time to you than to Stegosaurus." The answer is T. rex, and most adults get it
wrong because they picture all dinosaurs living at the same time.

**Are birds really dinosaurs?**
Yes. Birds evolved from small feathered dinosaurs, and scientists class them as living
dinosaurs. That's why "a rooster" is a fair answer to a dinosaur riddle.

**How many clues should a "What am I?" riddle have?**
Three works best. One clue is too easy to guess by luck, and five clues gets long enough that
younger kids lose track of the first one.

**Is Pteranodon a dinosaur?**
No. Pteranodon was a pterosaur, a flying reptile that lived alongside the dinosaurs. It's a
favorite trick answer for that reason.
