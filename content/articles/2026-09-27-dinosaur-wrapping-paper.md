# Dinosaur Wrapping Paper: 6 Christmas Prints, What One Sheet Covers, and When to Order

If there's a dinosaur kid on your list this year, the present is probably already
dinosaur-shaped. The paper can be too. We make six **dinosaur wrapping paper** designs, all
of them Christmas prints: gingerbread cookies, snow globes, T. rexes in Santa hats, and one
pink print with palm trees for anyone who spends Christmas somewhere warm.

This page covers all six, what you get in a pack, which boxes one sheet will actually cover,
and when to order so the paper turns up before wrapping night rather than after it.

## What's in a pack

Every design is sold the same way, and the details below are taken from the listings:

- **3 flat sheets** per pack, each **28.75″ × 19.75″** (73 × 50.2 cm)
- **70# uncoated text** paper with a **matte** finish
- Printed on one side, **white on the back**
- **$24.99** per pack of three, the same for every design
- Custom printed when you order, which is why it isn't a next-day delivery

Two things are worth knowing before you buy. These are sheets, not a roll, so you get three
pieces at a fixed size rather than a length you cut from. And 70# text is a
paper weight, not cardstock, so treat it like good wrapping paper rather than a gift box. The white back is
handy for writing a name straight onto the inside flap.

## Will one sheet cover the box?

This is the question the listings don't answer, so here is our rough math. To wrap a box in
the usual way, the paper has to go all the way around it with a little overlap, and reach
past each end far enough to fold down. For a box that is **L** long, **W** wide and **H**
tall, that means a piece about:

- **2 × (W + H) + 2″** in one direction, and
- **L + H + 1″** in the other.

A single sheet is 28.75″ × 19.75″. Here is how some common gift boxes come out:

| Box (L × W × H) | Paper needed | One sheet? |
|---|---|---|
| Book or jigsaw box, 10 × 8 × 2″ | 22″ × 13″ | Yes, with room to spare |
| Shirt box, 15 × 9.5 × 2″ | 25″ × 18″ | Yes |
| Kids' shoebox, 13 × 8 × 5″ | 28″ × 19″ | Yes, just (about ¾″ to spare) |
| Board game, 11 × 11 × 3″ | 30″ × 15″ | No, needs two sheets joined |
| Large toy box, 16 × 12 × 6″ | 38″ × 23″ | No, needs two sheets joined |

These are estimates for a neat wrap with ordinary folds. Measure your box before you start.
The short version: a pack of three sheets does about three book-, shirt- or shoebox-sized
presents, or one big toy box with a sheet left over for something small. If you're wrapping
everything under the tree in dinosaur paper, you'll want a few packs, and mixing two or three
designs looks deliberate rather than like you ran out.

When a box is too big for one sheet, lay two sheets side by side and join them with tape on
the white side before you start. On a busy all-over print like these, the seam is hard to spot.

## Wrapping ideas that make the paper go further

- **Match the tree.** If you've bought any of our
  [dinosaur Christmas ornaments](/collections/dinosaur-christmas-ornaments), pick the paper
  in the same colors. The navy Gingerbread Dinosaurs sits well next to a traditional tree, and
  Snow Globe Dinosaur next to a green-and-gold one. Our
  [ornaments guide](/blogs/blog/dinosaur-christmas-ornaments-guide) goes through the designs.
- **One print per person.** With six designs, each child can have their own paper, the same
  trick as giving everyone a different stocking. Nobody needs a tag to know which presents
  are theirs.
- **Save the offcuts.** A strip left over from a shirt box is enough to wrap a small
  stocking filler or cover a gift card envelope.
- **Plain ribbon.** The prints are busy. A single color of ribbon, picked from the paper,
  looks neater than a patterned bow.
