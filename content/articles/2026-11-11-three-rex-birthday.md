# Three Rex Birthday: A Dinosaur Party Plan for Three-Year-Olds Who Want to Play

Three is the first birthday your child will really be in charge of. They know it's their party, they have opinions
about the cake, and they can tell you which dinosaur is the best one (it's always T. rex). That's what makes a Three Rex
birthday so much fun: three-year-olds can finally play proper games, follow one simple rule, and roar on cue for the
photo.

They still get tired, overexcited and shy all in the same hour, though. Here's a full plan built around what
three-year-olds can actually do, from the invitations to the shirt.

## The Three Rex birthday plan at a glance

| Part | What works at three | Skip it |
|---|---|---|
| Length | About two hours, done before dinner | An all-afternoon party |
| Guests | A handful of friends their age, plus family | The whole preschool class, unless you have the helpers |
| Games | Four or five, each with one simple rule | Long games with teams or scorekeeping |
| Cake | A dinosaur cake they help choose, with a big number 3 | A cake they're not allowed to touch |
| Activity | One craft or "dig" they can take home | Anything that needs sitting still for 20 minutes |
| The outfit | A Three Rex shirt, plus matching grown-ups if you like | A costume that itches |
| Favors | Stickers, a coloring page, what they made | A bag of small plastic toys |

## Invitations: let the pun do the work

A few lines that fit on a card or a group text:

- *"Roar! Leo is turning THREE. Stomp on over to his Three Rex party."*
- *"Three Rex is on the loose! Join the herd for cake, games and a lot of roaring."*
- *"Our little dinosaur is three. Come dig for fossils, hunt for eggs and eat some cake."*

Put the start and end time on the invitation, and say whether siblings are welcome. Parents of three-year-olds often
have a baby in tow, and it helps them to know. If you're hosting a fossil dig, add "clothes that can get sandy."

## How long, and how many: the two decisions that matter

**Two hours is the sweet spot.** Most three-year-olds have dropped the morning nap, and many have dropped the
afternoon one too, but they still run out of steam. Two hours gives you time for games and cake without anyone falling
apart. A late-morning slot (10:30 to 12:30) or an early-afternoon one (2:00 to 4:00) both work.

**Keep the guest list small.** There's an old rule of thumb that you invite as many children as the birthday child's
age plus one. At three, that's four friends. You don't have to stick to it, but it's a good reminder: a party with
four friends and a few cousins is a lot more fun for a three-year-old than one with twenty kids they half know. If you
do invite the whole class, plan on one grown-up helper for every three or four children.

## A two-hour running order

1. **First 20 minutes: arrivals and the dig site.** Children turn up at different times and need a few minutes to warm
   up. Have the fossil dig (below) ready so the first arrivals have something to do straight away.
2. **Next 35 minutes: games.** Three or four short ones, back to back, with a grown-up leading.
3. **Next 15 minutes: a sit-down activity.** A simple craft or coloring gives everyone a breather before food.
4. **Next 25 minutes: food, then the cake and the song.**
5. **Last 25 minutes: free play, presents if you're opening them, favors and goodbyes.**

Three-year-olds who are tired get silly, then teary. If the energy tips, skip a game and go straight to the cake.

## Decorations: dinosaurs plus the number three

The whole theme is "dinosaurs plus a big 3," so repeat both.

- **A giant 3.** A number balloon, a 3 on the door and a 3 on the cake.
- **Green plus one bright color.** Green plates and balloons with orange or yellow say "dinosaur" without anything
  themed. If your child has a favorite color, let it be the bright one.
- **Footprint trail.** Big three-toed footprints cut from paper, taped from the front door to the party. Three-year-olds
  will follow them, and some will jump from print to print.
- **A volcano corner.** A cardboard volcano with orange and red tissue-paper "lava" makes a backdrop for the photos.
- **Let them help.** Three is the age when "I did it!" matters. Ask the birthday kid to stick on footprints or pick the
  balloons. They'll be prouder of the decorations than anyone.

## Three Rex cake ideas

Three-year-olds care about the cake, so it's worth letting them choose between two or three ideas:

- **The T. rex and the 3:** a frosted cake with a big number 3 candle and a washed toy T. rex standing guard. The toy
  goes home with the birthday kid.
- **The volcano cake:** a small cake stacked on a bigger one, frosted brown or green, with orange "lava" running down the
  sides.
- **Dinosaur dig cake:** chocolate cake with crushed cookie "dirt" on top and a couple of toy dinosaurs half buried.
  Easy to make, and the kids love digging the dinosaurs out.
- **Dinosaur egg cupcakes:** speckled candy eggs on a nest of green frosting. Easy to hand out, and no slicing.

For food, dinosaur names make everyday toddler food feel like party food: dino nuggets, a "herbivore" tray of fruit
and veg, "fossil" crackers and "lava" ketchup. For young guests, cut grapes and cherry tomatoes lengthwise into
quarters, and leave out whole nuts and popcorn.

## Three Rex party games that work at three

This is where a third birthday gets fun. Three-year-olds can follow one rule, take short turns and play a game more
than once. They still don't like losing, so pick games where everybody ends up with something. Our
[dinosaur party games guide](/blogs/blog/dinosaur-party-games) has twenty more, sorted by age.

- **Fossil dig:** a shallow bin of sand or dry rice with plastic dinosaurs, "bones" or painted rocks buried in it. Give
  each child a paintbrush and a spoon. Brushing the sand off like a real paleontologist keeps three-year-olds busy for
  ages, and it's the best way to start the party.
- **Dinosaur freeze dance:** play music, everyone stomps like a dinosaur, and when the music stops, they freeze. Call
  out a dinosaur each round ("freeze like a Stegosaurus!"). Nobody is out.
- **T. rex says:** like Simon Says, with dinosaur moves: "T. rex says stomp your feet," "T. rex says flap your
  pterodactyl wings," "T. rex says roar." At three, skip the "out" rule and just laugh at whoever moves.
- **Dinosaur egg hunt:** hide plastic eggs around the yard or living room, a little harder than you would for
  two-year-olds. Give each child a bag and a rule: "find five eggs, then help a friend." Put a sticker in each egg.
- **Feed the T. rex:** a cardboard box painted as a dinosaur with a big mouth cut out, and beanbags to toss in. Let
  three-year-olds take a big step back each time they score.

Plan four and keep one spare. Then end on a high: the cake is coming.

### A quiet craft: dinosaur crowns

Before food, sit everyone down to decorate a paper crown cut with dinosaur spikes along the top. Stickers, crayons and
a stapler are all you need, and every child leaves with something they made. Have a grown-up write names inside before
the party, because by the cake, all the crowns look the same.

## Three Rex birthday FAQ

**What is a Three Rex birthday?**
It's a dinosaur-themed third birthday that plays on "T. rex" and "three." The usual pieces are a big number 3, a T. rex
on the cake and invitations, and a THREE REX shirt for the birthday kid.

**How long should a third birthday party be?**
About two hours. That's long enough for games, food and cake, and short enough to end before anyone gets overtired.

**How many kids should I invite to a three-year-old's party?**
A common rule of thumb is their age plus one, so four friends, plus family. If you invite more, plan one grown-up helper
for every three or four children.

**What games can three-year-olds play?**
Games with one simple rule where everyone ends up with something: a fossil dig, freeze dance, "T. rex says," an egg hunt
and a beanbag toss. They can take short turns now, but they still don't like being "out."
