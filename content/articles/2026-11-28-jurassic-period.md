# The Jurassic Period: 56 Million Years of Giants, Plates and the First Birds

The **Jurassic Period** is the one with the famous name, and the name is a little misleading. Most of the stars of
the movies, *Tyrannosaurus*, *Velociraptor* and *Triceratops*, lived in the Cretaceous, long after it ended. The real
Jurassic belongs to *Stegosaurus*, *Allosaurus*, *Diplodocus* and *Brachiosaurus*: plated plant-eaters, big-clawed
hunters and the longest-necked animals that ever walked.

It lasted from about 201 million years ago to about 145 million years ago, roughly 56 million years. In that time a
single supercontinent began to pull apart, the sauropods grew into giants, and a small feathered dinosaur in what is
now Germany gave scientists their first look at how birds came to be.

This guide covers where the name comes from, how the period is divided, what the world looked like, which dinosaurs
lived when, the other animals that shared the planet, and how it all ended. There are kids' facts and an FAQ at the
bottom.

## Where the name "Jurassic" comes from

The name comes from rocks, not dinosaurs. In 1795 the explorer Alexander von Humboldt described the limestone of the
Jura Mountains, the range along the border of France and Switzerland, as *Jura-Kalkstein*. In 1829 the French
geologist Alexandre Brongniart formalized the idea as the *Terrains Jurassiques*, and the name stuck to the whole
stretch of time those rocks represent.

Older British books split the Jurassic into the **Lias**, **Dogger** and **Malm**, quarrymen's words for the rock
layers. Today those map roughly onto the Early, Middle and Late Jurassic. On geological maps the period's symbol is
simply **J**.

## When did the Jurassic Period start and end?

**The start, about 201.4 million years ago, is pinned down precisely.** Geologists mark it with a "golden spike", a
reference point in a real rock layer, at Kuhjoch in the mountains of western Austria. The marker is the first
appearance of a particular ammonite, *Psiloceras spelae*.

That date sits just after the **end-Triassic extinction**, one of the five great mass extinctions. As Pangaea began to
tear apart, enormous volcanic eruptions (the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province) poured out lava and carbon dioxide
across what are now four continents. Many of the crocodile-line reptiles that had dominated the Triassic disappeared.
The dinosaurs came through, and with their rivals gone, they took over.

**The end, about 145 million years ago, is fuzzier.** There was no catastrophe to mark it, and the base of the next
period, the Cretaceous, is still the only period boundary of the last half-billion years without an agreed golden
spike. The 145 figure is a working number with a few million years of uncertainty around it. (More on that in our
guide to the [Cretaceous Period](/blogs/blog/cretaceous-period).)

## Early, Middle and Late Jurassic: the 11 stages

Each part of the Jurassic is split into stages, most named for places in Europe where the rocks were first studied.
Ages are from the International Commission on Stratigraphy and rounded.

| Epoch | Stage | Began (million years ago) | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Early Jurassic** | Hettangian | 201.4 | Recovery after the end-Triassic extinction |
| | Sinemurian | 199.5 | *Scelidosaurus* in England, *Dilophosaurus* in Arizona |
| | Pliensbachian | 192.9 | *Cryolophosaurus* in Antarctica |
| | Toarcian | 184.2 | A low-oxygen "dead zone" event in the oceans |
| **Middle Jurassic** | Aalenian | 174.7 | The least-known stretch of dinosaur history |
| | Bajocian | 170.9 | Ammonites diversify rapidly |
| | Bathonian | 168.2 | *Megalosaurus* in Oxfordshire |
| | Callovian | 165.3 | *Liopleurodon* in the seas over England |
| **Late Jurassic** | Oxfordian | 161.5 | *Guanlong*, *Sinraptor*, *Anchiornis* in China |
| | Kimmeridgian | 154.8 | The Morrison Formation's giants |
| | Tithonian | 149.2 | *Archaeopteryx* at Solnhofen |

The Middle Jurassic is a genuine gap. Fewer rocks of that age that preserve land animals survive, so it is the part
of the dinosaurs' story with the fewest fossils, even though important things clearly happened in it: by the time the
record picks up again in the Late Jurassic, the big groups of dinosaurs were already well established.

## What the world looked like in the Jurassic

### Pangaea comes apart

At the start of the Jurassic almost all land was joined in **Pangaea**. Over the period it split along a line roughly
where the central Atlantic Ocean is today. North America pulled away from northwest Africa, the young Atlantic opened
as a narrow sea, and by the Late Jurassic the Gulf of Mexico was forming.

The result was two great landmasses: **Laurasia** in the north (North America, Europe and Asia) and **Gondwana** in the
south (South America, Africa, Antarctica, India and Australia), separated in the east by the **Tethys Ocean**. They
were still close enough for animals to move between them for much of the period, which is why very similar dinosaurs
turn up in Colorado and in Tanzania.

### A warm, green, ice-free planet

The Jurassic was warmer than today, with far more carbon dioxide in the air and little or no permanent ice at the
poles. As Pangaea broke up, moist ocean air reached deeper into the land, and the dry deserts of the Triassic gave
way to wetter, greener landscapes. Sea level rose through the period, flooding much of Europe into a shallow,
island-dotted sea. Much of what is now England, France and Germany sat underwater, which is why the region is so rich
in Jurassic marine fossils.

### No flowers, no grass

If you could stand in a Jurassic forest, the biggest surprise would be what's missing. There were **no flowering
plants** (they spread in the Cretaceous) and so no grass, no fruit trees and no flowers. Instead the land was covered
by **conifers** (relatives of today's monkey puzzle tree and redwoods), **cycads**, extinct cycad-like plants called
**bennettitaleans**, **ginkgos**, **tree ferns**, ordinary ferns and **horsetails**. The giant sauropods were eating
needles, fronds and tough leaves, which helps explain their huge guts and peg-like teeth.

## Dinosaurs of the Jurassic Period, by age

The Jurassic is when dinosaurs went from one successful group of reptiles to the rulers of every continent. Three
things stand out.

**The Early Jurassic** belonged to fairly lightly built meat-eaters and the first armored dinosaurs. *Dilophosaurus*,
with its twin head crests, hunted in what is now Arizona around 190 million years ago. In England, *Scelidosaurus* was
one of the earliest dinosaurs to carry rows of bony armor along its back, an early relative of both *Stegosaurus* and
*Ankylosaurus*.

**The Middle Jurassic** gave us the first dinosaur ever named. William Buckland described *Megalosaurus* from
Oxfordshire in 1824, before the word "dinosaur" even existed. In China, early stegosaurs like *Huayangosaurus* were
already walking around with plates and tail spikes.

**The Late Jurassic** is the classic Jurassic. The sauropods reached their peak, the plated stegosaurs and the big
allosaurs were everywhere, and the first birds appeared.

*Camptosaurus*, *Dryosaurus* and the small armored *Gargoyleosaurus*; and *Giraffatitan* from Tanzania, long filed as
a species of *Brachiosaurus* and now usually treated as its own genus. The species posts in our
[dinosaur facts blog](/blogs/dinosaur-facts) cover each of them.

### The Late Jurassic and the Morrison Formation

Most of the Late Jurassic dinosaurs people know come from one layer of rock: the **Morrison Formation**, mud, sand and
river deposits laid down roughly 156 to 147 million years ago across what is now Colorado, Utah, Wyoming, Montana and
neighboring states. It was a broad, seasonally dry floodplain crossed by rivers, with forests along the water.

The Morrison is where the 19th-century "Bone Wars" were fought. Starting in 1877, the rival paleontologists Othniel
Marsh and Edward Drinker Cope raced to dig and name its dinosaurs, sometimes sloppily, and between them they named
*Stegosaurus*, *Allosaurus*, *Diplodocus*, *Apatosaurus* and *Camarasaurus*. You can still see the rock at Dinosaur
National Monument, where a wall of bones remains in place, and at the Cleveland-Lloyd Quarry in Utah, which has
produced the remains of dozens of *Allosaurus*.

The Morrison is also home to one of paleontology's best comebacks. Marsh named *Brontosaurus* in 1879, it was folded
into *Apatosaurus* in 1903, and for most of the 20th century "there's no such thing as Brontosaurus" was a favorite
piece of trivia. Then in 2015, a large study of diplodocid skeletons concluded the two are different enough to be
separate genera again. Brontosaurus is back.

How the Morrison's top predator stacks up against the most famous meat-eater of all is its own story: see
[Allosaurus vs T. rex](/blogs/blog/allosaurus-vs-t-rex).

### The age of the sauropods

Few land animals have ever matched the Late Jurassic sauropods (only their Cretaceous cousins, the titanosaurs, clearly beat them). *Diplodocus* reached around
25 meters long, most of that neck and tail. *Brachiosaurus* held its head high like a giraffe. In China,
*Mamenchisaurus* had a neck that may have made up nearly half its length.

They could get that big partly because of how they were built. Like birds, sauropods had air sacs that ran into
their bones, making a huge skeleton much lighter than it looks. They didn't chew, so they didn't need heavy jaws; they
raked in plants and let their enormous guts do the work. And they laid eggs, which let them breed in large numbers
without the long pregnancies that limit big mammals.

### Stegosaurus is closer in time to you than to T. rex

*Stegosaurus* lived around 150 million years ago. *Tyrannosaurus rex* lived around 68 to 66 million years ago. That
puts more than 80 million years between them, more time than separates *T. rex* from you. The Jurassic and the
Cretaceous together are a far bigger stretch of time than people picture.

### The first birds

In 1861, two years after Darwin published *On the Origin of Species*, quarry workers near Solnhofen in Bavaria found
a fossil of an animal with feathered wings, teeth, clawed fingers and a long bony tail:
[*Archaeopteryx*](/blogs/dinosaur-facts/archaeopteryx-the-bridge-between-dinosaurs-and-birds). It lived
about 150 million years ago, on islands in a warm, shallow sea, and about a dozen skeletons have been found since.

For a long time it was the only feathered dinosaur anyone knew. Now we know of many. *Anchiornis*, from China about
160 million years ago, had feathers on all four limbs, and well-preserved fossils have let scientists work out its
colors: grey and black with a reddish crest. *Yi qi*, from the same time and region, had bat-like wings of skin. The
line between dinosaur and bird turns out to be a blurry one, and the Jurassic is where it blurs.

## Jurassic period animals beyond the dinosaurs

The dinosaurs had plenty of company, in the air, in the sea and underfoot.

| *Pterodactylus* | Pterosaur | Late Jurassic | The first pterosaur to be scientifically described (1809), from Solnhofen |
| *Dimorphodon* | Pterosaur | Early Jurassic | Big-headed flier found by Mary Anning in 1828 |
| *Ichthyosaurus* | Ichthyosaur | Early Jurassic | Dolphin-shaped reptile that gave birth to live young |
| *Ophthalmosaurus* | Ichthyosaur | Middle–Late Jurassic | Eyes among the largest of any vertebrate for its size |

| *Liopleurodon* | Pliosaur | Middle Jurassic | Big-jawed marine predator, often wildly exaggerated in size |
| *Metriorhynchus* | Sea crocodile | Middle–Late Jurassic | A crocodile relative with flippers and a tail fin |
| Ammonites | Mollusk | Whole period | Coiled-shell relatives of squid, used to date the rocks |

Mammals were already around, but small. Most were the size of a mouse or a rat, active at night, eating insects. The
Jurassic ones were more varied than once thought: there were swimmers, gliders and burrowers among them.

The seas are where the Jurassic is most famous in Britain. In the early 1800s Mary Anning, a fossil collector in
Lyme Regis on the Dorset coast, found some of the first ichthyosaur and plesiosaur skeletons ever recognized in the
cliffs there. That coast, now a World Heritage Site, is still known as the Jurassic Coast, and fossils still wash out
of it after every winter storm.

## How did the Jurassic Period end?

Quietly. Unlike the period before it and the period after it, the Jurassic did not end with a mass extinction. Some
groups declined at the end of the Late Jurassic, especially among the stegosaurs and some of the big sauropod
families, and sea levels fell for a while, but there was no single catastrophe.

The Cretaceous that followed was a continuation, not a restart. Sauropods carried on, birds diversified, and over the
next 79 million years flowering plants spread across the land. The story picks up in our
[Cretaceous Period guide](/blogs/blog/cretaceous-period).

## FAQ

**When was the Jurassic Period?**
From about 201.4 million years ago to about 145 million years ago. It's the middle period of the Mesozoic Era,
between the Triassic and the Cretaceous.

**How long did the Jurassic Period last?**
About 56 million years. The start is precisely dated; the end is uncertain by a few million years, because the
boundary with the Cretaceous has no agreed reference point.

**What dinosaurs lived in the Jurassic Period?**
*Stegosaurus*, *Allosaurus*, *Diplodocus*, *Apatosaurus*, *Brontosaurus*, *Brachiosaurus*, *Ceratosaurus* and
*Camarasaurus* in the Late Jurassic of North America; *Dilophosaurus*, *Scelidosaurus* and *Cryolophosaurus* in the
Early Jurassic; *Megalosaurus* in the Middle Jurassic; and *Archaeopteryx* and *Compsognathus* in Germany.

**Did T. rex live in the Jurassic Period?**
No. *Tyrannosaurus rex* lived at the very end of the Cretaceous, around 68 to 66 million years ago, more than 75
million years after the Jurassic ended. Its early relatives, such as *Guanlong*, did live in the Jurassic, but they
were much smaller.

**Did Velociraptor and Triceratops live in the Jurassic?**
No, both are Late Cretaceous animals. Despite the famous film, most of its best-known dinosaurs are from the
Cretaceous.

**What was the climate like in the Jurassic?**
Warmer than today, with high carbon dioxide and little or no ice at the poles. It became wetter as Pangaea broke up,
and sea levels rose through the period.

**Were there flowers in the Jurassic Period?**
No confirmed ones. The land was dominated by conifers, cycads, ginkgos, ferns and horsetails. Flowering plants spread
in the Cretaceous.

**What came before and after the Jurassic?**
The Triassic came before, and ended with a mass extinction about 201 million years ago. The Cretaceous came after,
starting about 145 million years ago and ending with the asteroid impact 66 million years ago.

**Why is it called the Jurassic?**
After the Jura Mountains on the France–Switzerland border, whose limestone layers were the first rocks of this age
to be described.

**What was the biggest dinosaur of the Jurassic?**
The sauropods. *Diplodocus* reached about 25 meters long, *Brachiosaurus* stood tallest, and relatives such as
*Supersaurus* may have been longer still. Size estimates for the biggest of them are based on partial skeletons and
vary between studies.
