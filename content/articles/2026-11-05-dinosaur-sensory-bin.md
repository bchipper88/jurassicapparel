# Dinosaur Sensory Bin: 8 Easy Ideas, Fillers by Age, and How to Keep the Mess in the Bin

A dinosaur sensory bin is a shallow tub, a filler to dig through, and a handful of toy dinosaurs hidden inside. That's
the whole idea, and it's why it works: a three-year-old who can't sit through a story will happily excavate the same
T. rex forty times. Below are eight bins that take ten minutes to set up, a table of fillers sorted by age (including a
taste-safe one for toddlers who still put everything in their mouths), and the few rules that keep the rice out of the
carpet.

## What you need for any dinosaur sensory bin

- **A bin.** An under-bed storage box with a lid is ideal: wide, shallow, and you can put the lid on and slide it away
  at the end. A roasting tin or a baking tray works for one child.
- **A filler.** Something to pour, scoop and bury things in. See the table below.
- **Dinosaurs.** Small plastic figures, 2–4 inches long, that you probably already own. Six to ten is plenty. Too many
  and it turns into sorting, not digging.
- **Tools.** Spoons, measuring cups, a funnel, a paintbrush, tongs. Kitchen drawer things are better than anything you
  can buy, because kids know what they're for.
- **A splat mat.** An old shower curtain or a flat sheet under the bin. Shake it outside when you're done.

## Dinosaur sensory bin fillers, by age

Pick the filler first, because it sets the age limit. Small dry fillers like rice and beans are choking hazards for
children under three, and that matters more than how good the bin looks.

| Filler | Best age | Mess | Feels like | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rolled oats | 1–3 | Low | Soft, dry | Taste-safe and cheap. The default for toddlers. |
| Cloud dough (heat-treated flour + oil) | 2+ | Medium | Silky, holds a shape | Molds like wet sand. Great for fossil digs. |
| Water + a few drops of dish soap | 2+ | High | Wet, bubbly | Outdoors or in the bath. Never leave a child alone with water. |
| Kinetic or play sand | 3+ | Medium | Damp sand | Holds footprints well. Not for mouthers. |
| Dry rice (plain or dyed green) | 3+ | High | Pours, rattles | Gets everywhere. Choking hazard under 3. |
| Dried black beans or chickpeas | 3+ | Medium | Heavy, clicky | Easier to sweep than rice. Choking hazard under 3. |
| Shredded paper or crinkle filler | 2+ | Low | Light, rustly | Good "nest" for egg hunts. |
| Real soil, pebbles and leaves | 4+ | High | Earthy | Outdoors only. The most realistic dig. |

**Skip water beads.** They're sold for sensory play, but they swell inside the body if swallowed, and US safety
regulators have warned parents about them. There's no bin idea here that needs them.

**A note on "taste-safe."** It means "won't hurt if a bit gets eaten," not "a snack." Raw flour can carry bacteria, so
heat it before you make cloud dough for a toddler: spread it on a tray and bake at about 350°F for 5 minutes, then let
it cool completely.

## 8 dinosaur sensory bin ideas

### 1. Fossil dig

The classic, and the one to start with. Bury your dinosaurs in cloud dough or kinetic sand, hand over a paintbrush and
a spoon, and let them excavate like a real paleontologist. A paintbrush is the important part: it slows kids down and
makes it feel like real work.

**Make it better:** bury salt dough "fossils" (dinosaur feet pressed into dough and baked; the recipe is in our
[dinosaur crafts guide](/blogs/blog/dinosaur-crafts)) alongside the toys, and ask them to match each fossil to the
dinosaur that made it.

**Best for:** 3–6. **Setup:** 10 minutes.

### 2. Dinosaur egg hatch

Freeze small dinosaurs inside water balloons the night before. In the morning, peel off the balloons and put the ice
"eggs" in a bin with warm water, spoons, squeeze bottles and a little salt. Kids work out for themselves that warm water
and salt melt the ice faster. It takes 20–40 minutes depending on the egg size, which is a long, quiet stretch for a
four-year-old.

If you want a coloring activity for when the eggs are done, the
[Hatching Dino Eggs coloring page](/products/hatching-dino-eggs-dinosaur-coloring-page-printable) has the same idea
on paper.

**Best for:** 3–7. **Setup:** 5 minutes, plus overnight freezing.

### 3. Toddler oat swamp

The taste-safe dinosaur sensory bin for toddlers. Fill a tray with rolled oats, add a few chunky dinosaurs (bigger than
a toilet-roll tube is wide, so they can't be a choking risk), and a big spoon and a cup. That's it. One-year-olds will
pour and dump for longer than you'd believe.

**Best for:** 1–3. **Setup:** 2 minutes.

### 4. Dino car wash

Muddy dinosaurs in, clean dinosaurs out. Mix a little cocoa powder or washable paint with water and dunk the dinosaurs.
Then set up a second tub of soapy water with toothbrushes, sponges and a towel. Scrubbing a Triceratops's horns is
surprisingly absorbing, and you end up with clean toys.

**Best for:** 2–6. **Setup:** 10 minutes. Outdoors or in the bath.

### 5. Volcano bin

Build a volcano out of play sand or cloud dough around a small cup. Put a tablespoon of baking soda in the cup with a
drop of red food coloring, place the dinosaurs around the base, and let them pour in vinegar. It erupts, the dinosaurs
flee, and they'll ask to do it again. Keep a jug of vinegar and a box of baking soda handy; you won't get away with
doing it once.

**Best for:** 3–8. **Setup:** 10 minutes. Have towels ready.

### 6. Dinosaur letter hunt

For kids learning letters. Write letters on small stones or bottle caps (or use foam letters) and bury them in rice or
beans with the dinosaurs. Each time they find a letter, they say it, or find the matching letter on an alphabet chart.
Our [ABC Dinos alphabet printable](/products/abc-dinos-dinosaur-alphabet-printable) has all 26 capitals with a baby
dinosaur on each one, and it works well taped to the wall behind the bin as the matching board.

**Best for:** 4–6. **Setup:** 15 minutes.

### 7. Big and small sort

Put a mix of big and small dinosaurs in a bin with two smaller containers labeled "big" and "small" (or "meat-eaters"
and "plant-eaters" for older kids). They dig, they decide, they sort. It's an easy way to slip in some real dinosaur
size facts: a Velociraptor was about the size of a turkey, and a Brachiosaurus could look over a three-story building.

If they want to see the real difference, the
[Dinosaur Size Chart printable](/products/dinosaur-size-chart-kids-dinosaur-poster-printable) lines up a child next
to a Velociraptor, Stegosaurus, Triceratops, T. rex and Brachiosaurus, drawn to approximate relative scale.

**Best for:** 3–7. **Setup:** 5 minutes.

### 8. Prehistoric swamp (small world)

The most elaborate bin and the one older kids like best. Use green-dyed rice or real soil as the ground, a shallow dish
of water as a lake, pebbles, real leaves and sprigs of rosemary or fern as plants, and a few sticks as fallen trees.
Then they set up the dinosaurs and tell stories. It's less about digging and more about pretend play.

**Best for:** 4–8. **Setup:** 20 minutes.

## How to dye rice green for a dinosaur sensory bin

Green rice looks like a swamp and photographs beautifully, which is why it's all over Pinterest.

1. Put 2 cups of uncooked rice in a zip-top bag or a jar with a lid.
2. Add 1 teaspoon of white vinegar and about 10 drops of green food coloring.
3. Seal and shake until every grain is colored.
4. Spread it on a baking tray lined with paper and let it dry for an hour or two.

Once it's completely dry, the color won't come off on hands. Stored in a sealed container, it keeps for months and
gets reused for every bin after this one.

## How to make cloud dough

Mix **8 cups of flour with 1 cup of vegetable or baby oil** with your hands until it holds together when squeezed but
crumbles when poked. For a toddler, heat-treat the flour first (see above) and use vegetable oil. Cocoa powder turns it
brown, like mud, and makes the fossil dig look more real.

## Keeping the mess in the bin

Every sensory bin article says "it's worth the mess." It's more useful to say how to have less of it.

- **Fill the bin a third of the way.** Overfilling is the main cause of spills.
- **Set two rules before you start:** the filler stays in the bin, and the dinosaurs can go out but the rice can't.
- **Sit them on the floor, not at a table.** It's a shorter drop, so it scatters less.
- **Dress for it.** Put them in clothes you don't mind getting dusty. A dinosaur tee from our
  [toddler dinosaur clothes](/collections/toddler-apparel) is in the spirit of things, and it'll wash.
- **End it on purpose.** Give a five-minute warning, then let them "put the dinosaurs to bed" by burying them, and put
  the lid on. Ending with a job beats ending with "stop."

## Running a dinosaur sensory bin with a class

- **One bin for every four children.** Any more and it becomes a fight over the T. rex.
- **Bring enough of each tool.** Four children, four spoons, four brushes.
- **Rotate in 10–15 minutes.** Sensory bins work best as one station among several.
- **Have a reward ready.** A [dinosaur sticker](/collections/dinosaur-stickers) for each "paleontologist" who finds all
  the fossils is a nice end to it. Our [dinosaur printables](/collections/dinosaur-printables) include an alphabet chart

## FAQ

### What do you put in a dinosaur sensory bin?

A filler (oats, cloud dough, kinetic sand, rice or dried beans), six to ten small toy dinosaurs, and some scooping
tools like spoons, cups and a paintbrush. Add extras for a theme: stones, leaves and water for a swamp, ice eggs for a
hatching bin, or letters for a learning bin.

### What age is a dinosaur sensory bin for?

From about one year old with a taste-safe filler like rolled oats and big dinosaurs, up to seven or eight for small-world
and science bins. Children under three shouldn't use rice, beans or small toys because they're choking hazards.

### Is a dinosaur sensory bin safe for toddlers?

Yes, with the right filler and supervision. Use rolled oats or heat-treated cloud dough, choose dinosaurs too big to
fit in the mouth, skip water beads entirely, and stay with them for the whole session.

### How do you make a fossil dig sensory bin?

Bury toy dinosaurs or salt dough fossils in cloud dough or kinetic sand. Give the child a paintbrush and a spoon, and
let them dig the fossils out slowly, the way paleontologists do. Matching each fossil to its dinosaur turns it into a
game.

### How long does a sensory bin keep a child busy?

For most preschoolers, 15 to 30 minutes at a time, longer for a new bin. Swapping one thing (a new tool, a few
different dinosaurs) makes an old bin feel new again.

### How do you store a sensory bin?

Use a storage box with a lid and keep everything dry. Dry fillers like rice, beans and oats last for months in a sealed
box. Throw away cloud dough or oats that get wet or smell off.
