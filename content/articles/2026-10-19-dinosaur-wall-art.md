## Dinosaur wall art by style

Five looks cover the whole range. Pick the look first and the rest of the decisions get easier.

### Vintage museum plates

These are the prints that look like they came out of an old natural history library, because some of
them did. The [Stegosaurus & Triceratops poster](/products/stegasaurus-triceratops-dinosaur-poster)
and the [Diplodocus poster](/products/diplodocus-dinosaur-poster) are digitally restored from
*The Dinosaurs of North America* (1896) by Othniel Charles Marsh, a U.S. Geological Survey report that
was one of the first big scientific surveys of American dinosaur fossils.

The [Trilobite](/products/trilobite-dinosaur-poster) and
[Pteranodon](/products/pteranodon-dinosaur-poster) posters are restored from the work of Heinrich
Harder (1858–1935), a German artist whose prehistoric paintings were printed on collectible cards
sold with chocolate. (A small honesty note: neither a trilobite nor a pteranodon is a dinosaur. A
trilobite is a sea creature that died out before the first dinosaurs appeared, and a pteranodon is a
flying reptile. They still hang very happily next to one.)

The newer [Velociraptor Natural History Plate](/products/velociraptor-natural-history-plate-dinosaur-poster)
is drawn in the same old scientific-illustration style, but with feathers, the way paleontologists now
think *Velociraptor mongoliensis* looked.

All four of the restored plates go up to 24″×36″, and the Trilobite and Pteranodon go all the way down
to 5″×7″ for a desk or shelf.

## Choosing a size and a frame

Our posters ship as prints. **Only the Fossil Hunter poster comes framed**, so for everything else
you'll want a frame, and the size you pick decides how easy that is.

| Poster size | Ratio | Frame situation |
|---|---|---|
| 8″×10″, 11″×14″, 16″×20″, 18″×24″, 24″×36″ | Standard US | Easy. Off-the-shelf frames everywhere |
| 12″×18″, 20″×30″ | 2:3 | Common in poster frames; check before you buy |
| 12″×16″, 24″×32″ | 3:4 | Less common. Plan on a custom or online frame |
| 10″×10″ to 18″×18″ | Square | Easy at 12″ and 16″; other squares vary |
| A2, A1 (Diplodocus, Trilobite, Pteranodon) | Metric | Buy an A-size frame; US frames won't fit |

Two rules of thumb make the size choice simple:

- **Over furniture, go for about two-thirds of its width.** A 60″ sofa wants roughly 40″ of art. That
  means one 24″×36″ print hung landscape, or three 16″×20″ prints in a row.
- **Hang the center at about eye level,** around 57″ from the floor, which is what galleries use. In a
  kid's room, drop it so the center sits at their eye level instead.

All the posters are printed on thick matte paper, 10.3 mil and about 189–192 g/m². Matte means no
glare under a bedroom lamp.
